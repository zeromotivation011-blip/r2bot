# R2BOT Kids: product review and roadmap

*26 November 2026. Combines seven expert reviews: child engagement, learning science, visualisation and simulation, robotics coverage, schools and parents, career inspiration, and product quality. Every finding was checked against the code in this repository. The seven themed worlds and Robo Sumo are being built now, so they are counted as planned.*

How to read the effort labels: **S** is a few days, **M** is one to three weeks, **L** is a month or more.

---

## 1. Verdict

R2BOT Kids already has more real robotics in it than almost any children's product. It has a real physics simulator, games built on real control and AI systems, 478 machine-checked activities, one shared robot and one rewards engine, plus children's privacy that is properly designed. It does not yet feel like **one product**. The tabs still lead to the 2025 "Robot World". The best content (the Skill Galaxy, the 3D episodes, the Physics Lab) is hard or impossible to find. Stories and Code Lab pay out a different currency from everything else. And the full course exists only for ages 5–7, which is three quarters of the age range left without one. The next job is not to add more. It is to **merge, connect and finish**: one map, one robot, one currency, one voice, a path for every age band, and grown-up surfaces that schools and parents can trust. After that, the features that will spark a lasting interest are making the robot visible (inside view, what it senses, replays), connecting play to real robots and real jobs, and taking robotics off the screen (home builds, AR, real hardware).

## 2. What is already strong

- **The robotics is real.** The Rapier physics has motor torque, wheel slip, sand and ice. The games are built on real systems: PID drone, inverse-kinematics arm, a circuit solver, k-NN, A* mapping, swarms. Each game has four tiers (Cadet to Legend), so one game works from age 7 to 16.
- **A large, verified content base.** The Skill Galaxy has 478 activities (400 hand-written) across 10 tracks × 4 age bands on 26 interactive templates. `verify.ts` proves every solution works. Control, electronics, mechanics and safety/ethics are taught in real depth.
- **One foundation for everything.** One robot shape (`RobotDesign`) is used by the Workshop, Hangar, AR and Physics Lab. One progress store. One rewards function (`reportActivity`) for XP, badges, unlocks and quests.
- **Humane motivation rules.** Learning earns rewards and time does not. Personal bests instead of rankings, no punishing streaks, nothing bought.
- **A strong Level 1 course.** 24 sessions, each Episode → Build → Code → Challenge → Check, with voiced 3D episodes. Check distractors are written from real misconceptions. Levels 2–4 already have full story outlines.
- **Children's privacy done properly.** A child joins with a code and a nickname. There is no email, password or birthday, the token is stored as a hash, and the class snapshot holds counters only.
- **A school base.** Classes, assignments, a "stuck" insight that never singles out one child, 40-minute teacher guides for Level 1, teacher training with a certificate, a strong server-rendered `/kids/schools` page, and honest curriculum-alignment notes.

## 3. The ten biggest gaps

1. **Navigation points at the old product.** The tabs are Stories, Workshop, Code Lab and Robot World. Course, Skill Galaxy, Games and Hangar have no tab, and the Galaxy has no inbound link and is not in the sitemap. *Children cannot find most of what has been built.*
2. **Two reward economies.** Stories, Code Lab, Robot World and Boss Challenges pay old "stars" and never call `reportActivity`. A child can finish them all and their level never moves. *A reward loop that breaks teaches a child that effort does not count.*
3. **No course for ages 8–17.** Only Level 1 is playable, and Physics Lab arenas and 3D episodes exist for Level 1 only. *The ages at which a lasting interest in STEM usually forms have no path through the product.*
4. **The site is not yet safe for children's pages.** GA4, the adult footer, the auth modal and unguarded links to the adult site load on every `/kids` page and inside the app. The privacy page says "we do not knowingly collect data from children under 13". *This blocks the app-store kids categories and contradicts what we tell schools.*
5. **Progress can vanish.** Everything is stored only in the browser. A new phone, cleared storage, Safari's storage eviction or a shared school tablet wipes the robot, level and badges. *Children cannot "come back next month" if their robot is gone.*
6. **Three robots, two episode systems, two coding languages.** `/kids/my-robot`, the Robot World parts and `RobotDesign` are separate robots. The weaker browser-voiced stories have a tab while the better Piper-voiced 3D episodes are hidden. Code Lab and the Physics Lab use different block languages. *Ownership and learning are spread thin instead of building on each other.*
7. **The 5–7 band cannot read the rewards, and the 8+ band has no concept-level learning.** Rewards are silent text cards. "Mastery" means "did each activity once". There is no spaced review, no hint ladder and no adaptivity beyond age. *Young children miss the joy; older children miss the challenge.*
8. **Teachers and parents see the wrong numbers.** The class dashboard, CSV and parents page still count Robot World zones and minutes played. Course, Galaxy and game progress is invisible. *Schools adopt products they can report from.*
9. **Play is not connected to real robots or real jobs.** About 400 `realWorld` lines are plain text. Careers appear in the Galaxy 0 times. There are no role models and no link to the main R2BOT Academy. *Interest becomes a future only when a child can picture the job.*
10. **The rewards ladder and the social layer stop short.** Nothing unlocks after level 25, the 9 weekly quests are the same for every age (and some cannot be completed), and there is no co-op, gifting, surprise or seasonal layer. *Teens leave exactly where the ladder ends.*

## 4. The roadmap

Each item is listed once, with similar proposals from the reviews merged. Within each wave the items run from most to least valuable.

### Wave 1: Make it feel like one perfect product (merges and fixes)

1. **Kids-safe pages and an honest kids privacy notice (S).** No GA4, adult footer, ProgressBanner, AuthModal or view counter anywhere under `/kids` or in the app. Add an "R2BOT Kids" section to `/privacy` covering what a class stores, how long it is kept, how it is deleted, the subprocessors, and the consent basis (COPPA school authorisation, GDPR-K, India DPDP). Remove the "under 13" contradiction. *Why:* nothing else matters to a school or an app store until this is fixed. *Reuses:* `app/layout.tsx` (path check next to HideOnEmbed), `app/privacy/page.tsx`, ParentNote wording.
2. **Parental gate on every exit and adult feature (S).** "Hold for 3 seconds" or "type this number in words" before "R2BOT for grown-ups", Parents, Schools, Share to Classroom, outbound `target=_blank` links, the camera/AR prompt and Ask Spark. *Why:* required by the store kids categories, and it stops a child wandering into the adult Co-pilot. *Reuses:* KidsShell footer, AssignmentsPanelClient, ARViewClient entry.
3. **One Universe navigation (M).** Five tabs: **Home** (the Universe map), **Learn** (your band's planet course plus the Skill Galaxy), **Play** (games plus the 7 Worlds), **Garage** (your robot), **Me** (badges, bests, passport). Each opens at the edge of the child's band. *Why:* the child finally finds the best 80% of the product. *Reuses:* KidsShell TABS, UniverseMapClient, PLANETS/GAMES/WORLDS.
4. **Link in the Skill Galaxy (S).** Links from the home hero, the map, the end screen of every course session ("practise this"), and Hangar mastery. Add `/kids/galaxy` and the 10 track pages to `app/sitemap.ts`. *Why:* 400 hand-written activities currently sit behind no door. *Reuses:* app/kids/page.tsx, sitemap.ts.
5. **One currency: route every surface through `reportActivity` (M).** Story Theatre, Code Lab, Robot World levels and `/kids/challenge` all earn XP, badges and quest progress. Convert old stars to XP once, on load. Remove the 4-second polling in KidsShell. *Why:* every finished thing must move the child's level, or the habit loop breaks. *Reuses:* rewards.ts, the getProgress migration.
6. **Retire Robot World into the Universe and clean up the names (M).** Re-home the 6 zones as Planet Spark warm-ups or Explorers Galaxy activities, and redirect (308) `/kids/world/*`. Settle the URLs so `/kids/world` and `/kids/worlds` never both exist. Call the XP level **Rank**, so "Level" means only the course level. Rename the "Star Cadet" rank so it does not clash with the Cadet tier. Move `/kids/activities` to `/kids/printables`. *Why:* a 6-year-old meets bands, tiers, ranks, planets, moons, zones and worlds today, which is too many named systems. *Reuses:* next.config redirects, worlds.ts.
7. **One robot, one Garage (M).** Merge the Workshop (3D build plus AR), Hangar (paint, skins, badges) and My Robot (robot card) into one place, all built on `RobotDesign`. Robot World's earned parts become Workshop unlocks. *Why:* "my robot" is the emotional core, and three robots dilute it. *Reuses:* robot-design.ts, robot3d.ts, HangarSceneClient, WorkshopClient, ARViewClient, cosmetics.ts.
8. **Episodes Theatre on the Piper voices (M).** `/kids/stories` becomes the cinema for the 24 voiced 3D episodes, each with "play this mission" below it. Re-voice the 4 browser-voiced stories or retire them. *Why:* the best "wow" is hidden on a noindex lab URL. *Reuses:* EpisodePlayerClient, public/kids/audio, the StoryPlayer quiz panel.
9. **Band-aware "Your next mission" card (S).** Every visit shows one clear next step. Ages 5–7 get the next Level 1 session. Ages 8–17, until their planet ships, get a curated "Prologue season" of Galaxy activities plus a game at the right tier. *Why:* a visible next step is the strongest return driver, and 8–17-year-olds currently meet "arriving soon". *Reuses:* ContinueCardClient, MissionStripClient, byBand(), GAMES tiers.
10. **Fix the quest and XP engine (S).** Make every quest completable: report `bugfix` from debug-program activities and `build` from Garage saves (at most once a day), and hide course-only quests from children with no open session. Split the quest pool by band and grow it to about 40, plus a bonus 4th quest. Give game XP only for a first win or an improvement, not for replays. Require a minimum score for the drone Legend tier (26% of random tunes win today). *Why:* an impossible quest or a lucky badge teaches a child that the system is unfair. *Reuses:* currentQuests/questStatus, GameShellClient.finish, ActivityPlayerClient's `improved` rule.
11. **An unlock at every level up to 60 (S).** About 45 new unlocks: hangar props, robot emotes, nameplate frames, eye styles, sound packs, World skins, and teen-credible items (a blueprint skin, a telemetry HUD theme). Mastery badges unlock items too. *Why:* levels 26–60 are empty today, and that is where teens decide to stay. *Reuses:* UNLOCKS, cosmetics.ts, looks.ts, avatars.tsx.
12. **Robot Passport: back up progress without personal data (M).** "Save my explorer" gives four friendly words plus a QR code (for example MAGNET-OTTER-NOVA-7). The server stores progress keyed by the code's SHA-256. Typing the code on a new device restores everything, and a parent can print it as a passport card. *Why:* lets a child come back across devices without an email. *Reuses:* classroom token hashing, readJsonCapped, encodeDesign. *Needs a migration; it must degrade safely until Ravi runs it.*
13. **Teachers see the whole Universe (M).** Rebuild `classroom-stats`, the CSV and the "stuck" signal on course sessions, Galaxy activities by track, course checks and game tiers. Sync stars and attempts per activity in compact form. Stop reporting minutes played. *Why:* teachers following the course currently see almost nothing. *Reuses:* classroom-stats.ts, ClassSnapshot (8 KB budget), AssignmentsPanelClient.
14. **Rebuild the parent hub (M).** Behind the parental gate it shows: what the child learned this week in plain words (from badge "because you can…" lines), sessions, Galaxy tracks, the robot, one "try this at home" idea, and "cheer" stickers that appear on the child's Garage wall. It also holds the controls: a daily time budget with a gentle wind-down, quiet hours, and on/off switches for Ask Spark, camera/AR and the external link. There is an opt-in weekly email to the **parent** through `captureLead()`. Navy and orange design, global examples (remove the Maggi and CBSE-only content). *Why:* parents are the main reason children under 10 come back, and they trust controls they can set. *Reuses:* ParentReportClient, levelInfo, captureLead.
15. **Immersive play mode and thumb-sized controls (S).** Hide the tab bar during games, Galaxy activities and lab arenas, with one Exit button that confirms if there is unsaved work. Make every control at least 48 px (tier buttons and the "?" button are 35–40 px today). *Why:* QA found taps on the tab bar wiping circuits. *Reuses:* the KidsShell `bare` flag, GameShellClient.
16. **Lite 3D and no black screens (S).** Move the episode engine's weak-device detection into the shared `stage.ts` (DPR 1, 512 px shadows, no antialiasing), render only when something moves, handle `webglcontextlost`, and fall back to a poster. Add a "Lite 3D" switch. *Why:* the global audience is mostly on budget Android phones. *Reuses:* director.ts adaptQuality, HangarPoster.
17. **Offline that covers the new product (S).** Update the `kids-sw.js` precache list to include the course, Galaxy, Garage, play and sim assets, and bump the version. *Why:* patchy school Wi-Fi breaks exactly the newest surfaces. *Reuses:* public/kids-sw.js.
18. **Content hygiene (S).** Remove the 78 template samples from the live Galaxy, or review and promote them. Gate the `/kids/lab/templates/*` test benches. Add canonical and noindex to `/kids/challenge/*`. Make the game count honest (Sumo appears on the home page but is excluded from the sitemap). Add the 4 missing realWorld links. Re-test the Level 1 Session 15 sand tuning and the Session 9–12 pen offset, both listed in OPEN_QUESTIONS. Reconcile `kids-games-qa-todo.md`. *Why:* children must never hit unreviewed content or an impossible step.
19. **Brand and bundle pass (S).** Remove the purple and indigo backgrounds (Workshop, AR, Code Lab, SensorPanel, sim buttons, the legacy screens, about 39 hits). Give `/kids` its own OG image. Stop KidsShell from statically loading 38.8 KB of Robot World tips. Keep one Spark character, which the coming Ask Spark mentor replaces rather than adds to. *Reuses:* docs/kids-design.md tokens, dynamic().
20. **One "For grown-ups" hub, and an updated brief (S).** `/kids/grown-ups` fronts Parents, Schools, Teach, Classroom and Join, and is the single gated footer link. Update CLAUDE.md §6 to list Course, Galaxy, Games, Garage, Lab, Teach and Worlds. *Why:* six adult entry points with no front door, and a stale brief steers future work toward the old surfaces.
21. **One block language (L).** Port the 24 Code Lab missions to Blockly on a flat grid arena, using the picture blocks for ages 5–7, then retire the custom editor. *Why:* one vocabulary that grows from 5 to 17 is a ladder; two vocabularies are a reset. *Reuses:* blockly-json.ts, PictureProgramClient, the code-lab reference solutions as tests.

### Wave 2: Must-have features

22. **Rewards you can hear and see (M).** For ages 5–7, Spark speaks every quest, badge and level-up, and quests become picture cards. Every level-up becomes a 2–3 second ceremony: the XP bar fills, the rank stamps in, the new item drops onto the 3D robot with "Equip now", and the robot strikes a victory pose. Distinct sounds per reward type, and it respects reduced motion. *Why:* the core loop is silent for pre-readers and flat for everyone else. *Reuses:* RewardToastClient, REWARD_EVENT, speakNatural/Piper, HangarSceneClient, Confetti.
23. **Courses for Levels 2–4, with teacher guides (L).** Write Gearon, then Nova Prime, then Apex to the Level 1 standard. Build each session mostly from existing parts: an episode, a verified sim arena, 2–3 concept-tagged Galaxy activities, a game tier as the challenge, and the 3-question check. Extend the teacher guides, the GrownUps drawer and certificates to every level, and retitle `/kids/teach` "all ages". Until a level ships, show "coming in <month>" with a waitlist through `captureLead()`. *Why:* the single largest gap. *Reuses:* levels.ts outlines, SessionRunnerClient, sim verify, episode engine, guides.ts.
24. **Finish the explorer-friendly world games first (L, already planned).** Ship Safety Inspector, Ocean Rescue, Bio-Bots and Smart Farm (all `minBand: explorers`) before the builder-only worlds. *Why:* only 1 of the 8 built games targets ages 5–7.
25. **Access settings that actually exist (M).** Text size, high contrast, a dyslexia-friendly mode, "read everything to me" (every option and every piece of feedback in every template), colour-blind safe cues, switch/keyboard scanning, and "more time". Add an accessibility checklist per template to `verify.ts`. *Why:* the school guide already promises these, and non-readers are the whole Explorers band. *Reuses:* speech.ts, kids-shell.css, the levels.ts access spec.
26. **Hint ladder and "Spark shows me" (M).** After a failed try the child gets three steps: the goal highlighted, then a targeted hint, then a worked solution followed by a matching "your turn" twin. Hints are spoken. Using them caps stars at 2 but still earns XP. *Why:* stops 8–12-year-olds quitting on Ohm's law or PID. *Reuses:* the verified `solution` fields, CheckResult.feedback, worked-example.ts.
27. **Concept tags and real mastery (M).** Every activity gets `concepts: ConceptId[]`, enforced by `verify.ts`. A concept counts as "secure" after 3 first-try successes across 2 different templates within 21 days, and shows as "fading" after that. Each concept appears as a constellation in the Garage, and teachers see the same grid. *Why:* every adaptive, review and reporting feature depends on this. *Reuses:* CONCEPT_SPIRAL, ActivityRecord.
28. **Recharge missions: spaced review (S).** The daily missions become 1 new item, 1 "recharge" item on a fading concept (using a different template), and 1 stretch item. In the story, the robot's battery cells drain for fading skills. Skipping a day costs nothing. *Why:* retrieval practice over time is what makes learning stick. *Reuses:* dailyMissions/nextActivities.
29. **Launch Check placement and adaptive pacing (M).** A 6–8 item placement mission sets a starting point per track. Three first-try wins in a row offers "skip to Ace?". Two failures in a row offers an easier version of the same concept. Teachers can lock a child's band. *Why:* "too easy" and "I'm bad at this" are the two first impressions that lose a child. *Reuses:* nextActivities, tiers.ts, worked-example.ts.
30. **Misconception capture and the Class Mind Map (M).** Record which wrong answer was chosen and sync counts only, never raw answers. The teacher sees "9 of 28 think a bigger driven gear goes faster: run the 5-minute reteach in the Session 14 guide". *Why:* the misconception data was carefully written and is currently thrown away, and this is what makes schools rely on the product. *Reuses:* teacher.misconceptions, CheckStepClient, class snapshot, guides.ts.
31. **Gradebook by concept and a standards page (M).** A grid of children × concepts with evidence click-through, CSV/PDF export, and a server-rendered `/kids/teach/standards` page plus PDF per band built from `mapping.ts` (CSTA, NGSS, NEP, UK KS1–3). *Why:* `mapping.ts` is imported by nothing today, and heads of department need it to timetable the course. *Reuses:* mapping.ts, teach/pdf.ts, rosterCsv.
32. **Robodex: collect real robots (M).** Activities, episodes and world games "discover" real robot cards (a Mars rover, a surgical arm, a milking robot, an ocean glider). Each card has a band-aware back: a voiced story for 5–7, specs (DoF, sensors, autonomy) plus an Atlas `/robots/<slug>` link for teens, and "who builds this?". Progress shows per World ("Ocean Lab 7/12"). Earned only by doing, never bought or drawn at random. *Why:* collecting is the strongest pull for ages 6–12, and every card is a real machine. *Reuses:* `realWorld` fields (add a checked `robotSlug`), lib/robots-data.ts (136 robots; leave out its India-only fields), Garage shelf.
33. **A robot that grows with the child (M).** New RobotDesign slots unlocked by play: power pack, motor type, a second sensor, an arm with joints, and drone, submarine and humanoid chassis from the matching World. A spec sheet shows mass, runtime, top speed and reach. The robot visibly carries what it learned (the line sensor glows after Line Rider) and greets the child with the next mission. It never decays or makes the child feel guilty. *Why:* one evolving robot is the strongest retention loop, and today it stops growing at age 7. *Reuses:* robot-design.ts, robot-spec.ts, capabilitiesOf, Garage.
34. **Meet the Makers and the Career Constellation (M).** About 20 original, diverse role-model characters from every continent (ROV pilot, surgical-robot engineer, agri-robot technician, rover driver, safety tester, prosthetics designer). Each has a voiced 60-second story for young children and "how I got here" for teens. A career map lights up the skills the child already has, and "Try my job" opens the matching game. Add a `careers` badge track. *Why:* careers appear 0 times in the Galaxy, and seeing someone like you in the job is what turns interest into a future. *Reuses:* episode engine, cast.ts, Piper, ConstellationClient, the world track.
35. **Graduate to R2BOT (S).** When a child reaches the Engineers band, a "Your next step" screen maps their mastery onto Quick Lessons, Academy Forge/Edge, Robot Projects, the ROS 2 Playground and Career Paths. Under-13s see these links only in the parent/teacher view. *Why:* today there is no link at all from `/kids` to the main platform. *Reuses:* liveShorts(), /academy, /careers, /ros2.
36. **X-ray and explode view (M).** A slider pulls the robot apart. An X-ray toggle shows the battery, the "brain" board, the motor driver, the motors and colour-coded wires, all derived from the robot's capabilities. Tap a wire to watch a signal travel ("distance sensor → brain: 34 cm"). Engineers see voltages and currents. *Why:* seeing inside a machine is the classic "aha" moment. *Reuses:* RobotHandles.slots, ROBOT_PARTS_3D copy, capabilitiesOf.
37. **Robot's-eye sensor view (M).** A picture-in-picture "what my robot senses": all 20 ultrasonic rays with hit points, a low-res head-camera image, the line-sensor patch, a bumper flash, and a "raw numbers" mode for teens. *Why:* the gap between what we see and what the robot senses is the clearest idea in robotics, and the engine already computes it. *Reuses:* engine.ts computeSensors (expose `beams[]`), renderer.ts, SensorPanel.
38. **Flight Recorder: replay, ghost and graphs (M).** Every sim and game run is recorded, with a scrub timeline, a ghost of your best run next to the live one, and graphs of sensor readings, wheel speed against ground speed (slip shaded) and motor %. "Why did it fail?" marks the moment on the timeline. *Why:* turns failure into discovery, and "beat yourself" gives the pull of competition with no ranking. *Reuses:* the deterministic engine, renderer snapshots, drone-hover plot.ts, personalBests.
39. **Battery and power in the simulator (M).** A charge model (drain from motor current, electronics and mass), a live battery bar, robots that can run flat, plus a Power Lab: cells in series and parallel, voltage sag, solar sizing, return-to-charger routes. *Why:* makes the planned "Energy Budget" sessions playable, and energy is the constraint every real engineer fights. *Reuses:* engine back-EMF model, the Circuit City solver.
40. **Class Power-Up: a co-op class goal (M).** The teacher sets a shared goal ("Relight Lantern Hill: 300 activities this month"). Every child's work fills one meter on the projector and on home screens. "Who helped this week" shows as ticks with no counts, and milestones unlock a class cosmetic and a class certificate. *Why:* social motivation with no ranking, and a weekly reason for teachers to assign R2BOT. *Reuses:* classroom sync, ClassMissionsClient, certificates. *Needs a migration column.*
41. **Shared-device mode (L).** On a lab tablet, the child taps their nickname (5–7-year-olds use an emoji picture-password) and "Done" clears the device for the next child. Progress is stored per member. Start by namespacing the storage key per member. *Why:* 40 children on 10 tablets currently overwrite each other's robots. *Reuses:* kids-progress.ts, membership tokens, JoinClient.
42. **Present mode for the projector (M).** Any session, episode, sim or replay opens full-screen with big type, the 5/10/20/5-minute lesson timer, the join code and QR in a corner, slow motion, freeze-and-draw, "ask the class" prompts from the teacher notes, and a hands-up vote step for the check. *Why:* the "works with one projector" promise has no presenter view yet. *Reuses:* LESSON_SHAPE, episode fullscreen code, SimWorkbench speed, JoinCard.
43. **School Trust & Privacy Pack (M).** A downloadable PDF generated from code: data inventory (every ClassSnapshot field, who sees it, retention, hosting), deletion flows, subprocessors, COPPA/GDPR-K/UK AADC/DPDP notes and a template parent letter. *Why:* schools adopt after a data review, and the facts already exist in the code. *Reuses:* classroom.ts, teach/pdf.ts.
44. **Make It at Home: kitchen-table builds (M).** Rebuild the 7 old printables into about 30 home and class builds linked to tracks and worlds: cardboard gripper, bristlebot, paper-cup echo test, syringe-hydraulic arm, lemon LED. Each has an adult-safety note, a "try one change" fair test and a link to the matching real robot card. An "I built it" check-in (a question, not a photo upload) counts as a `project`. *Why:* making something with your own hands turns screen interest into identity, and it costs families nothing. *Reuses:* the `project` kind and q-project-1, L1_PROJECTS, printables.
45. **My Robotics Portfolio and certificates for everything (M).** The Garage collects projects, replays, robot snapshots, design answers and badges by skill. Teens add one-line reflections. Export to PDF. Certificates for every level, every World ("Ocean Lab Specialist") and every milestone, each listing the skills shown. Teachers can bulk-print a class set with the school name and signature. *Why:* a portfolio is something a teen can show a school or club, and certificates are what goes on the fridge. *Reuses:* CertificateButtonClient, jspdf, MyRobot canvas share, 'I can' objectives. Local or class-only by default, as SHARE_RULE already says.

### Wave 3: Delight and depth

46. **The world restores as you play (M).** Planet Spark gains a festival light for each finished session and the fountain relights at session 24. Each World visibly upgrades as its activities are done. *Reuses:* sessionsDoneOn, UniverseMapClient, the levels.ts acts.
47. **Secret and tiered badges (S).** Bronze, silver and gold tiers for skill badges, plus 10–15 hidden "???" badges with riddle hints that reward curiosity and productive failure: Test Pilot, Short-Circuit Scientist, Fair Tester. *Reuses:* UNIVERSE_BADGES, the game debrief branches.
48. **Daily Discovery Crate (S).** The first finished activity of the day opens a crate with a Robodex card, a voiced fact, a prop or a secret mission. Contents are chosen from the date and band. No streak. *Reuses:* the week-key seeding, UNLOCKS.
49. **Mission Seasons tied to real robotics moments (M).** 2–4 week seasons themed on real events (World Oceans Day, World Space Week, Hour of Code, Mars landing anniversaries), each with season quests, a map decoration and a cosmetic that stays earnable afterwards, so nobody is shamed by FOMO. *Reuses:* the quest pool with date ranges, WORLDS.
50. **Arena and Puzzle Maker with share codes (M).** Inventors and Engineers build an arena, maze or circuit puzzle and must "prove it" by solving it themselves. It gets a share code. Class shelves are teacher-moderated, and the maker sees only "3 explorers solved your puzzle". *Why:* moving from player to designer is what engineers do. *Reuses:* arena.ts, sim verify, encodeDesign, the template verifiers.
51. **Robot League seasons and the Sumo tournament (L).** Every 6–8 weeks a season challenge awards criteria badges (Mission Complete, Efficient Code, Safe Robot, Creative Design, Great Notebook). The teacher runs a deterministic robot-vs-robot bracket on the projector with "what made this robot win", under robot names rather than children's. *Founder decision needed:* whether a class bracket fits the no-comparison rule. *Reuses:* sumo-arena, Rapier determinism, classroom assignments.
52. **Robot Postcards (M).** Send a classmate or family member your robot with a preset message and a sticker (no free text), optionally gifting a duplicate card. Received postcards pin to the Garage wall. *Reuses:* encodeDesign, safeRobotName, member tokens.
53. **A Day in the Life, one shift per World (M).** A Maker briefs the child, three chained tasks from that World's game and activities follow, and the shift ends with "You did what a medical robotics engineer does". *Reuses:* the 7 world games, Makers, UNLOCKS.
54. **AR Missions: your robot in your room (L).** Drive your robot on the real floor and take on missions: park on the rug, find something red (colour sampled on the device), drive a square and see the odometry error. Frames never leave the device. *Reuses:* ARViewClient hit-test and magic window, Workshop DriveInput.
55. **Real robots in 3D, with life-size AR (L).** About 12 brand-free, procedural archetypes (6-axis arm, warehouse AMR, quadruped, rover, quadcopter, humanoid, surgical arm, ROV, harvester, station arm, vacuum, self-driving sensor rig). Each can be orbited, exploded and X-rayed, plays a job loop, and can be placed at real size next to the child. *Reuses:* robot3d primitives, arm-factory kinematics, X-ray code, ARViewClient.
56. **See the robot think (M).** A live overlay shows the running block and sensor values, Sumo's state machine, the A* frontier growing above the maze, and the k-NN neighbours drawn as lines. *Reuses:* interpreter.ts, sumo brain.ts, maze planners, robot-brain.
57. **Watch a robot learn (L).** Set a reward, press Train, and see generation 1, 10 and 50 side by side with a rising fitness curve. Teens tune mutation and reward, then watch reward hacking happen, which is also a Robot Guardians lesson. *Reuses:* headless Rapier stepping, the Flight Recorder graphs, the ethics-dilemma template.
58. **Humanoid Lab: make it walk (L).** Build a biped, then move the centre of mass over the support foot, tune ankle balance against pushes, and design a gait that survives a slope. *Why:* humanoids are the most visible robots in 2026 media and currently a blank. *Reuses:* the sim, the drone PID UI, GameShell tiers.
59. **Build-a-Hand: grippers, prosthetics, soft robots (M).** Design a hand for an egg, a bottle, a sponge and a key (2-finger, 3-finger, suction or soft). At Legend tier, drive a prosthetic hand from a muscle-like signal. Feeds into Robot Hospital. *Reuses:* arm-factory, signal-graph, threshold-tuner.
60. **Motor Garage (M).** Bench-test a DC gearmotor, servo, stepper and brushless motor with a PWM sweep, then pick the right one for each job. Teens add an H-bridge. The chosen motor changes the sim. *Reuses:* motor-math and gear-train templates, robot-spec drive curve.
61. **Vision Lab: how a camera robot sees (M).** The live on-device camera view goes from pixels to colour mask to blob and centroid to "steer to the red ball", with a ladder from "robot eye spy" up to distance-from-blob-size feeding a P controller. *Reuses:* ARViewClient camera pipeline, threshold-tuner, train-mini.
62. **Where Am I? localisation (M).** A cloud of guesses (particles) collapses as the robot senses walls. Symmetric rooms fool it. At the top tier the child maps and localises at once, which is SLAM explained by playing. *Reuses:* Maze Mapper grid, the sim distance model.
63. **Drone Academy (L).** Choose frame, props, motors and battery, and see thrust-to-weight and flight time. Then 3-axis flight, waypoint missions, wind, and crop-coverage paths for Smart Farm. *Reuses:* drone-hover, the battery model, swarm-reef.
64. **ROS Junior and Blocks → Python (L).** Split the robot into nodes (camera, brain, wheels) connected by topics, and watch messages flow. Add a safety node that overrides speed. A live read-only Python view of the child's Blockly program appears in Level 3 and becomes editable in Level 4. Ends with the hand-off to the ROS 2 Playground. *Reuses:* flowchart-builder, blockly-json.ts, Monaco, /ros2.
65. **Robot Spotter and Robots Near You (S).** Weekly home quests ("find 3 sensors in your kitchen", "spot a robot on your street"). Tap "found it" or take a photo that stays on the device, then classify the find as sense, think or act. Earns a "Spotted in the wild" stamp. Includes phone-sensor experiments: record the accelerometer and see it on a signal graph. *Reuses:* spot-in-scene, classify-sort, signal-graph, Robodex.
66. **Hindi and Spanish R2BOT Kids (L).** UI, Level 1, Galaxy intros and feedback, Spark voice lines and teacher PDFs, with a class language setting and reciprocal hreflang. *Why:* early grades in India and Latin America are taught in those languages. *Reuses:* lib/hreflang.ts, the Piper pipeline, the existing ES/HI workflow.
67. **Teacher onboarding wizard and school pilot kit (M).** Three questions (grade, devices, lesson length) pick the band, plan and present mode, print the join card and parent letter, and assign week 1. Pilots add an anonymous class-level pre/post "I could be a robotics engineer" survey and an automatic impact report. *Why:* a first lesson that takes over about 15 minutes to prepare loses the teacher, and the survey measures the founder's real goal: interest. *Reuses:* ClassroomClient, LessonPlanPanel, PilotFormClient, pdf.ts.
68. **Robot Report: weekly kids robot news (M).** One or two real stories a week, **written and checked by a person**, at three reading levels and read aloud, each with a Galaxy link, a one-question check and a teacher discussion card. *Do not* pipe the machine-summarised adult news in directly. *Reuses:* the news table as a source, Piper, pdf.ts.

### Wave 4: Future

69. **Real Robot Link (L).** "Run on my robot" sends the same Blockly program over WebSerial or Web Bluetooth to a micro:bit or Arduino running a small open firmware. Hidden where unsupported. *Reuses:* the block contract, interpreter, Capacitor app.
70. **Make it real: digital twin of a starter kit (L).** A generic parts list (no prices or affiliate links), an exploded-view assembly, a wiring diagram drawn from the X-ray wires, code export, and a "sim vs real" checklist. Lays the groundwork for the kits option in §8 without gating anything.
71. **Chip Lab (L).** A virtual microcontroller with pins, digital vs analog input, PWM, loop timing and an e-stop interrupt, with blocks shown next to text code.
72. **Robot Factory Tycoon (L).** Design a production line (conveyors, arms, cobots, vision inspection) and balance throughput, bottlenecks, energy and safety.
73. **Exo Lab and HRI Studio (M each).** Fit an exoskeleton to a worker lifting boxes. Give the Face Screen head expressions, gaze, greetings and personal-space rules, then test them on simulated visitors (the Face Screen currently adds mass and does nothing).
74. **Kid-level Robopedia `/kids/explain/<concept>` (L).** The beginner rung of each Atlas entry rewritten for ages 8–12, with one embedded Galaxy activity, indexable. Record each page in `lib/canonical-map.ts` so it does not compete with the Atlas.
75. **Pair mode and Career Compass (S each).** Driver and navigator roles with a half-time swap for one device per pair. An on-device "your robotics strengths" profile with three matching career families, shown to parents and teachers as conversation starters.

## 8. Risks

- **Privacy and children's law.** Today GA4 and adult scripts run on child-directed pages, and the privacy page contradicts the product. Fix both before any school pilot or app-store submission (COPPA, GDPR-K, UK AADC, India DPDP). New server-side features (Passport, class goals, postcards, puzzle codes) must keep to the rules: no personal data, hashed tokens, preset text only, counters not raw answers. The camera stays on the device and asks only after a button press.
- **App store policy.** Apple Kids and Google Play Families need no third-party analytics, a parental gate before external links and settings, and no unguarded route into the adult site. The `apps/kids-mobile` README already lists these blockers; none is closed yet.
- **Trust promises.** The school guide promises large text, high contrast, switch access and K–12 lesson plans that do not exist yet. Build them or soften the claims before pilots.
- **Performance.** Budget Android phones are the core global device. Unshared 3D quality settings, always-on render loops and the AR camera at 2× DPR will cause stutter, heat and black screens. Every new 3D feature must use the shared Lite tier and stay procedural, with no large model files.
- **Cost.** Piper voices are pre-generated (19 MB already), so audio size will grow with Levels 2–4 and new languages; cache it in the service worker selectively. Server storage for Passport and class goals is small. Ask Spark AI is the only per-use cost, so keep it behind its flag and a daily cap. Resend's ~100/day limit matters once parent digests start.
- **Content quality.** Unreviewed template samples are live today. Kids news must be written by a person, never machine-summarised. Translations need native review before shipping.
- **Scope.** Seventy-five ideas could bury the product. Wave 1 is mostly wiring of things that already exist, and it should ship before any new game.
- **Migrations.** Passport, class goals and shared-device profiles need new tables. Ravi runs each migration by hand, so the code must work before he does.

### 9.2 Missing features

#### Wave 1 additions (trust, safety and the ability to see what is working)

76. **Privacy-safe learning telemetry (M).** No child action changes. The team gets aggregate counters only: activity started, finished, or abandoned at step N; hint used; tier chosen. Counts are kept per template, band and day, with no device ID, no stored IP and no cookie, and any count below 10 is suppressed. *Why:* once GA4 is gone (#1), nobody can see where children lose interest, and "perfect" cannot be reached without knowing that. *Precedent:* privacy-first education apps that count events without identifiers, which is what the children's app-store categories accept. *Reuses:* `reportActivity` (already the single choke point), `lib/rate-limit.ts`, a counters table. *Needs a migration; it must be a no-op until Ravi runs it.*
77. **An interest North Star (S).** Define and track the founder's real goal, not page views. The measures: week-4 return rate per band, the share of activities started **voluntarily** (not assigned), an "I want to learn more about this" tap at the end of each World, and the pilot pre/post survey (#67). *Why:* XP totals measure compliance, and voluntary return measures interest. *Precedent:* learning products that report "returned without being assigned" separately from assigned work. *Reuses:* #76, #67, World end screens.
78. **Ask Spark safety suite and a disclosure protocol (M).** The child sees no change until something goes wrong. Then, if a child writes that they are being hurt, are scared, or want to hurt themselves, Spark answers kindly, tells them to talk to a trusted adult now, and shows a region-aware helpline list **without** a parental gate. The engineering side is a red-team set of about 300 prompts in EN/HI/ES (personal details, self-harm, abuse disclosure, weapons, jailbreaks, homework cheating, scary robot questions) that runs in CI against `safety.ts` and the system prompt. No transcripts are retained, and teachers get a per-class Spark switch. *Why:* an AI mentor talking to 5-year-olds is the product's highest-severity risk, and `safety.ts` is a keyword list that redirects to robotics. It has no path for a child who is telling us something real. *Precedent:* child-focused AI tutors that publish safety evaluations and send disclosures to a trusted adult. *Reuses:* `lib/kids/ai/spark/safety.ts`, `faq.ts` and `offline.ts` (the helpline card works offline), the `KIDS_AI_ENABLED` flag.
79. **Physical safety rules for home and class builds (S).** Every Make It at Home build (#44) carries a hazard tag (button/coin cell, lithium pack, hot glue, blade, small parts, mains power: never), a minimum band and an "adult required" flag, and `verify.ts` refuses a build without them. Button cells are never used in an Explorers build. *Why:* swallowed button cells are a serious injury risk for young children, and a single incident would end school trust. *Precedent:* small-parts and battery warnings on toy and craft-kit packaging. *Reuses:* printables, curriculum safety fields, the parent note.
80. **Data deletion and expiry for families and schools (S).** A parent can press "Delete everything on this device" and "Delete my Passport". A teacher gets an end-of-year class purge, and class data expires automatically after about 13 months with no activity. *Why:* the Passport (#12) and class goals (#40) create server data about children, which brings GDPR-K, DPDP and COPPA deletion duties. The Trust Pack (#43) needs a real flow to describe. *Reuses:* classroom deletion code, the parent hub (#14).
81. **Guards that can see the kids product (S).** Add `/kids/course/1`, `/kids/course/1/1`, `/kids/galaxy`, one Galaxy activity, `/kids/play/drone-hover` and `/kids/hangar` to the URL list in `site-audit.yml`, and bump `--min` to match. Add a `check-kids-content.mjs` to `live-checks.yml` that asserts every `/kids/play/*` page serves 400 or more words of server HTML and every Galaxy track page lists its activities. *Why:* §16 of the brief warns that checks which only test existence miss empty pages. Today only 5 kids URLs are audited, and none of them is the new Universe. *Reuses:* `scripts/check-atlas-content.mjs`, `unlighthouse.config.js` floors.
82. **Expert review and a playtest cadence (S, process).** Every activity, episode, Robodex card and Maker story carries `reviewedBy` (robotics expert plus an educator) and `reviewedAt`, and `verify.ts` reports anything unreviewed. Once a month, about 5 children per band play a build while someone watches. Notes go into `kids-games-qa-todo.md`, and a session is not "done" until children have played it. *Why:* `verify.ts` proves solutions work, not that a child understands or enjoys them, and 478 activities carry no review record. *Precedent:* children's game studios playtest every milestone with the target age. *Reuses:* `verify.ts`, `OPEN_QUESTIONS`.

#### Wave 2 additions (audiences the review leaves out)

83. **Teen mode: an "R2BOT Lab" look for Engineers (M).** When the band is Engineers, the shell drops the "Kids" wordmark and the cartoon avatar defaults. It uses the main R2BOT typography, the telemetry HUD theme and spec-sheet language, and gives a separate home-screen name and icon inside the app. *Why:* a 15-year-old will not open something called "Kids" in front of friends, and 14–17 is where career choices are made. Today the whole band sits inside a product branded for 5-year-olds. *Precedent:* learning platforms that re-skin their teen tier and drop the childlike branding. *Reuses:* `docs/kids-design.md` tokens, `bands.ts`, #11's teen cosmetics, Graduate to R2BOT (#35). *Founder decision:* whether Engineers should live in the main R2BOT Academy instead.
84. **Clubs, homeschoolers and libraries (M).** In Club mode a leader runs a mixed-age group where each member keeps their own band, and weekly 60–90-minute session plans replace 40-minute periods. A homeschool parent creates a "family class" for siblings with the same tools a teacher has. Libraries and community centres get a printable drop-in session kit. *Why:* after-school clubs are where robotics interest is sustained year after year, and most schools timetable little or no robotics. The classroom model assumes one teacher, one grade and one period. *Precedent:* volunteer-led coding and robotics clubs run from libraries worldwide. *Reuses:* classroom, `classroom-plan.ts`, guides, join cards.
85. **Unplugged lessons for classrooms with no devices (M).** Each Level 1 session gets a no-device version: printable command cards, a "human robot" floor-grid game, sense–think–act sorting cards and a gear demo with paper gears. These suit a teacher with only a projector, or with nothing at all. *Why:* the brief says "anywhere on Earth", and many classrooms in the target regions have one device or none. Without this, those classrooms get no robotics at all. *Precedent:* unplugged computing activities are a standard part of primary curricula. *Reuses:* printables, `teach/pdf.ts`, the picture-block icon set, `guides.ts`.
86. **Robot Maths: "the maths inside" (M).** Each concept shows the maths it uses, at the child's band: counting steps and left/right for Explorers; coordinates, angles and headings for Builders; ratios, percentages and graphs for Inventors; and vectors, trigonometry for a 2-joint arm, and averaging noisy sensors for Engineers. Each has a matching Galaxy activity. *Why:* fear of maths is a common reason teenagers rule out engineering, and robotics makes maths visibly useful. Coverage is missing today. *Precedent:* applied-maths curricula that teach angles through turtle or robot motion. *Reuses:* `motor-math`, `gear-train`, `threshold-tuner`, `signal-graph`, arm-factory inverse kinematics.
87. **Inclusion and representation pass (S).** Audit the cast, the Makers (#34), the voice genders and the avatars: skin tones, hair types, head coverings, glasses, hearing aids and a wheelchair-user avatar. Avoid gendered default colours and use names from every continent. Make sure Inventors content offers care, medical and environment framings alongside Sumo and Rover. *Why:* interest in engineering among girls often drops around ages 11–13, and children decide early whether robotics is "for people like me". *Precedent:* toy and media audits for representation. *Reuses:* `avatars.tsx`, `cast.ts`, the Makers, `worlds.ts`.
88. **Connected robots and robot cybersecurity (M).** Inventors and Engineers send commands over a lossy link and watch latency and dropped messages break a teleoperated robot. In Robot Guardians they find a robot with a default password and fix it with an update. *Why:* almost every real robot is networked, cybersecurity is a fast-growing robotics job, and coverage is missing. *Reuses:* Orbit Repair teleoperation delay, ROS Junior (#64) topics, the ethics-dilemma template.
89. **Calm and sensory settings (S).** Separate switches for sound effects, music and voice, a no-confetti calm mode, untimed play and a fixed "what's next" strip. These are part of #25, but they are called out here for autistic and ADHD children. *Why:* sensory overload makes a child leave in the first minute. *Reuses:* `kids-shell.css`, `playSound`, reduced-motion handling.
90. **Family profiles at home (M).** Two or three siblings on one tablet each pick their own profile (Explorers use an emoji picture-password). The parent hub shows each child, and a "play together" mission pairs an older sibling as navigator. *Why:* siblings overwrite each other's robots at home exactly as classmates do in #41. Older siblings teaching younger ones is a strong interest seed for both. *Reuses:* do #41's per-member storage namespacing as per-profile namespacing first, so one change serves both.

#### Wave 3 additions (depth)

91. **Team Challenge season and a route to competitions (M).** Club and class teams of 3–5 take roles (builder, coder, tester, notebook keeper), keep an engineering notebook from a template, and present at a showcase judged on criteria. A parent- and teacher-view page, "Find a robotics club or competition near you", is curated for every region. *Why:* team robotics competitions are one of the best-documented routes from early interest to an engineering degree. *Precedent:* international youth robotics leagues that judge design, teamwork and notebooks, not only scores. *Reuses:* #51 criteria badges, the portfolio (#45), assignments.
92. **Design Bench: CAD and fabrication (L).** Design a bracket, wheel hub or gripper finger from 3D primitives, check that it fits the robot, then export a 3D-printable mesh or a cardboard/laser cutting template as a PDF. *Why:* designing a physical part is how many engineers start, and many schools and libraries now have a 3D printer that sits idle. *Reuses:* robot3d primitives; `STLExporter` ships inside the installed three.js examples, so no new package is needed; `teach/pdf.ts`.
93. **Robot Time Machine: history and future (S–M).** A voiced timeline runs from ancient automata to factory arms, Mars rovers and today's humanoids, and each stop is a Robodex card. The last stop asks the child to design a robot for a 2040 problem (climate, ageing, oceans) and saves it to the portfolio. *Why:* seeing how young the field is tells a child they can still shape it. *Reuses:* the `world` track (history is in scope), Robodex (#32), episode engine. Build it inside `/kids`; do not revive the parked `/history`.

### 9.3 Missing risks

- **Rewards crowding out interest.** Heavy contingent rewards (XP, crates, unlocks) can reduce a child's own interest in the activity once the rewards stop, which is the overjustification effect and the opposite of the founder's goal. *Mitigation:* make rewards informational ("you can now…") rather than payment. Prefer surprise to promised rewards. Keep an unrewarded sandbox (Physics Lab, Workshop free build). Judge every gamification change by voluntary return (#77), not by XP earned.
- **A child telling Spark something serious.** This is covered by #78. Until it ships, keep Ask Spark behind its flag for Explorers.
- **Teen drop-off because of the branding.** Covered by #83. Without it, the Engineers band and the career goal fail together.
- **Accessibility law, not just the school guide.** The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025, and the US ADA Title II web rule brings WCAG 2.1 AA deadlines for public schools in 2026–27. Public school procurement will ask for an accessibility conformance report. Add one to the Trust Pack (#43).
- **Physical harm from home builds.** Covered by #79. It has to land before #44 ships, not after.
- **Sustainability of a free product with 90+ items.** Levels 2–4 × 3 languages × audio × teacher guides is a maintenance load that grows with every release, carried by a very small team with no revenue. *Mitigation:* reuse first (each new session built from existing episodes, arenas and activities, as #23 already says). Gate new games behind Wave 1 being finished. Track content debt (unreviewed items from #82) as a number.
- **Combat framing.** Sumo and "battle" language can narrow who feels welcome. Frame Sumo as engineering (traction, centre of mass, pushing force), and balance it with care, rescue and environment worlds in every band (#87).
