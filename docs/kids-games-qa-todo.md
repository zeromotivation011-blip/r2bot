## circuit-city
- BLOCKER (reproduced in browser at 1280x800, and in the solver): a short circuit leaves the battery permanently flat, and every tier then blames the child's correct wiring. Repro on Cadet: wire the top and bottom rows, add a wire from (2,2) to (2,3) across the battery, wait about 6 s, remove that wire, add the switch and turn it on. The house stays dark and the battery reads 0.0 V. Test then says 'The house stayed dark because the path is not one complete loop…' even though the loop is complete (levels.ts:272). Screenshot: /tmp/circuit-city-qa/shots/d-cadet-deadbatt.png. Cause: drain() in CircuitCityGameClient.tsx:146-151, called from the loop at about :220-229, removes 18% charge per second until the current falls below SHORT_A (charge about 0.02, EMF about 0.7 V). Clear board keeps the drained charge on purpose (:552). The only way to recover is a 'Fit fresh batteries' button (:976) inside the panel of the locked battery, and nothing tells the child it exists. No Spark message says the battery is flat after the short ends.
- Same root cause, wrong science taught at Pilot. A correct parallel ladder (three rungs, 220 Ω lamps) on a drained battery (charge 0.05 or 0.02) fails, and the 'why' says 'The lamps are in series — one single path…' (levels.ts:300-301). The check `same && currents[0] < 0.01` cannot tell series wiring from equal-but-low parallel currents. Checked with the real solver: the ladder passes at charge 1 and 0.3, and at 0.05 gets 5.5 mA each plus the series explanation. Ace (levels.ts:330, 'No current reaches the sign… check it has a complete loop') and Legend (:374) mislead the same way. Fix: detect a flat battery (battV or charge) before any wiring diagnosis, and say so in Spark and the debrief with a one-tap 'new batteries' action.
- The Legend tier is not genuinely hard. Hints are free and do not affect stars or score (the star calculation at CircuitCityGameClient.tsx:~584-590 ignores hintLevel). Legend hint 2 gives the exact design: LDR on top, 10 kΩ below, 3 V threshold, 'ON when BELOW' (levels.ts:~150-152). Hint 3 gives the full LED branch topology and 100 Ω. The Ohm's-law helper (:~1020) also computes the LED resistor. The live goals panel plus the sky slider then turn the rest into trial and error. Tests cost nothing either: attempts only lower the score by 40 each, never the stars. Checked with the solver: that exact recipe scores quality 1, so 3 stars. A 16-year-old can copy the hints and get 3 stars without designing anything. Suggest gating or costing the hints, at least on Ace and Legend (e.g. hint 2+ caps stars), and hiding the exact numbers at Legend.
- Phone usability (390x844, partly in the shared layout): the fixed bottom tab bar `nav.kids-tabbar` covers the bottom rows of the 366x274 board once the board is scrolled into view (elementFromPoint over the canvas hit kids-tab links about 60 times). A Playwright tap on the board's lower area navigated to /kids, /kids/code-lab and /kids/world, and the circuit was lost because board state is not kept. The game should leave bottom space under its board or toolbar (or the shell should pad for the tab bar). Also in landscape at 844x390 the canvas is 672x504, taller than the viewport, so the board cannot be seen or dragged across in one view. makeView (render.ts:29-31) sizes by width only and should also cap by available height.
- Scientific accuracy nit: the Legend sweep's light levels and labels are unrealistic. 'Noon · 1000 lux' (levels.ts:237) and the slider's 'day 1000' are indoor light levels; real daylight is about 10,000 to 100,000 lux. 'Dusk 100 lux' is also loose. Either relabel the steps (e.g. 'Indoor daylight', 'Dim room') or say the sensor sits behind a window or shade, so the numbers children learn are true.
- Scientific accuracy nit: the part description says 'Streetlight: An LED lamp with its own 220 Ω resistor inside' (CircuitCityGameClient.tsx:55), but the Cadet house lamp is a lamp part with ohms: 47 (levels.ts:53). A child who inspects the house is told the wrong resistor value. Also, an LED at 35 mA pops after 0.45 s (circuit.ts:92, GameClient:205); real 5 mm LEDs survive 30–40 mA for a long time and wear out rather than popping. That is acceptable as a game rule, but the pop text should not suggest 35 mA destroys an LED instantly (only the direct-to-battery case, hundreds of mA, is realistic).
- Shared, outside the game's folders: the GameShell tier tabs (Cadet, Pilot, Ace, Legend) are 40 px tall (Ace is 47x40) and the '?' button is 35x44, under the 44 px minimum, in components/kids/ui/GameShellClient.tsx:108-131.
- Verified OK, no action: page HTTP 200, 1,027 words of server HTML in <article>, canonical https://www.r2bot.in/kids/play/circuit-city, index/follow, JSON-LD present. The GameShell renders, and finish is reached on a pass; the builder's runs reached the results dialog. Spamming Test 6 times ran only 1 test. 60 random taps on the board caused no errors. 60 fps idle, and 0 rAF callbacks after client-side navigation away, so no leak. No page errors in the console. Formulas checked: the divider is 3.75 V at 100 lux and 2.25 V at 10 lux, (6−2)/0.015 ≈ 267 Ω, and (4.5−3)/0.015 = 100 Ω. Series of three LEDs at 6 V gives 0 mA and parallel gives 17 mA each.

## robot-brain
- LEGEND IS TRIVIAL (fails the 'genuinely hard' bar). Reproduced at 390x844 and 1280x800: on Legend, press '✨ Sample' then 'Add' twice per class (no drawing at all), pick 'Engineered features' and k=5, then take the exam. It scores 23/24 (96%), and choosing the obvious quiz answer gives 3 stars. The run never needs any data cleaning, augmentation or validation iteration. A headless simulation with tsx against brain.ts/tiers.ts gives the factory data alone (3 wrong labels left in) with features at k=5 24/24 on the exam, and at k=3 23/24. Root causes: (a) Sample drawings (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:274-281) come from the same generator and VARIATION family as the test and exam sets (symbols.ts generateDrawing, tiers.ts makeTestSet), so they are ideal training data. (b) The engineered features (brain.ts:136-180) already cancel the exam's shift, scale and specks, so the 'wild' variation (symbols.ts:83) is no challenge once features are on. (c) Legend counts Sample examples as 'own' drawings (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:216). Suggested fixes: don't count Sample toward Legend's minPerClass, or draw samples from a different style than the exam. Make the exam really need rotation robustness or augmentation, e.g. wider rotation, stretch or partial strokes that features alone don't fix. Raise the first-star target to about 90%.
- THE LEGEND 'ONE-SHOT' EXAM CAN BE RETAKEN ON THE SAME PARCELS, which undoes the held-out-test lesson. After the exam, 'End round' or 'Play again' goes back through GameShell restart. CACHE (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:56,178-201) keeps every example, and tierTestSet uses the fixed seed 4421 (tiers.ts:173), so the identical 24 parcels come back. The debrief has just shown thumbnails and notes of exactly which parcels failed ('tilted 21°, moved left, with 3 smudges'). Confirmed in the browser: after Play again, 'Take the final exam (one shot!)' is enabled again with 24/80 examples kept. Pilot and Ace have the same leak: the test can be run without limit and the debrief says 'Add examples that look like the parcels I got wrong' (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:389). That is tuning on the test set, which contradicts the page's 'Held-out test data' paragraph (page.tsx 'A model must be scored on examples it never saw') and Pilot's goal text 'your robot has never seen them' (tiers.ts:63). Fix: give each exam attempt its own seed, e.g. testSeed plus the attempt number. Or lock the exam per session and hide the failed exam parcels until the round ends. For Pilot, re-test on a fresh seed.
- THE ACE BIASED-DATA LESSON CAN BE SKIPPED, AND THE DEBRIEF THEN SAYS SOMETHING FALSE. The first debrief blames the wrong labels ('Some training examples had the WRONG label… delete or relabel them'). Simulation shows deleting the 2 wrong labels alone leaves the score at 10/15 (67%, unchanged). Adding 3 hand-drawn → and fixing nothing gave 15/15 in the browser (shots ace-norelabel-*). The perfect-score message PERFECT[2] then says 'You fixed the data — correct labels and enough of every class' (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:71), which is untrue because the wrong labels are still there. The real cause is class imbalance: only one tiny → (tiers.ts:139-155). Fix: give the wrong labels real influence, e.g. move them next to the test →s or use k=1/3. Or gate 3 stars on no wrong labels remaining, and make PERFECT[2] state what the child actually changed.
- CADET AND PILOT ARE TOO EASY BY THE 'NOTHING BASIC' BAR. Simulated: ONE Sample per class scores 8/8 at Cadet and 12/12 at Pilot (features, k=3). Three crude hand-drawn arrows or circles per class also gave 100% in both browser runs. Pilot's '75% on strangers' handwriting' never pushes the player. Consider giving Pilot a more varied test set (VARIATION closer to wild) or showing raw pixels by default, so variety in the training data actually matters.
- THE LEGEND OVERFITTING QUESTION IS GIVEAWAY MULTIPLE CHOICE (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:164-174). The right option is the only plausible one and literally contains the word 'overfitting'. The other two ('training set too big', '100% proves the brain is perfect') are strawmen, so the 3rd star does not test understanding for 14–17-year-olds. Suggestion: ask for a prediction based on the child's own experiment log, e.g. 'which row will do worst on the exam?', or use options that are close to each other.
- LEGEND 2ND-STAR THRESHOLD IS HIDDEN BY ROUNDING (tiers.ts:180, stars [0.85, 0.92, 0.92]). With 24 parcels, 22/24 = 91.7% falls below 0.92, so 2 stars really needs 23/24 (95.8%). Nothing in the UI says so. Use 0.9 or state the parcel count.
- SCIENTIFIC ACCURACY, MINOR: CAUSE_LESSON.position (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:78) says 'moved, small or tilted drawings look different. Try engineered features', which implies the engineered features handle tilt. They don't. The 10x10 grid is not rotation-normalised and the direction histogram (brain.ts:157-171) changes with rotation. Only position and scale are normalised. Reword this, or add rotation normalisation.
- TRAINING ACCURACY BARELY VARIES WITH k, so the experiment log cannot show k's effect on overfitting. With the distance weight 1/(d+0.08) (brain.ts:236), a training example's own vote weighs 12.5, which almost always wins. Training accuracy reads 96–100% at every k (sim and browser log: 'features 5 24 96% 88%'). Yet QUIZ.explain and the page say 'a larger k' shrinks the gap. Fix: compute training accuracy leave-one-out as a separate column, or reduce the self-weight.
- PAGE COPY DOESN'T MATCH THE DATA: the Ace tier card (page.tsx:76) says 'nine ←, seven ✕ and only one tiny →, plus a few labels that are simply wrong'. The UI shows 11 examples under ←, because 2 → drawings are mislabelled ← (tiers.ts:148-153). Say '11 ← (2 of them really →)' or similar.
- MOBILE UX AT 390x844: the drawing pad starts at y≈744 (below the fold, shot start-390.png), and the Live brain bars plus 'Closest examples' sit below the pad. While drawing, a child can see neither the live confidence bars nor Sorta's scene, so the 'guesses live while you draw' feature is invisible on phones. Suggest a compact live-bar strip above or overlaying the pad at narrow widths.
- SMALL NAMING BUGS IN EXAMPLE NUMBERING: relabel() computes n from every example with that label, any source (RobotBrainGameClient.tsx:292). After a child adds their own →, a relabelled intern drawing can be called 'the intern's 5th → example' when only 3 intern → exist. addExample (line 259) counts current items, so delete-then-add produces duplicate names ('your 3rd ← drawing' twice). Neighbour and debrief text then points at the wrong drawing.

Server HTML is 964 words in <article>. Canonical is https://www.r2bot.in/kids/play/robot-brain with 2 JSON-LD blocks. About 61 fps during runs at both sizes. No rAF leak after client-side navigation to /kids (0 calls/s). The run pauses while the tab is hidden. It survives resize/rotate mid-run (844x390, 320x640) and tier switching mid-run. Keyboard-only Cadet works (1/2, S, Enter). No console errors (only an unrelated Permissions-Policy 'bluetooth' header warning). Game tap targets are ≥44px. ## mars-rover
- [HIGH, keyboard/a11y] MarsRoverGameClient.tsx:453-473: the window keydown handler takes over Enter, ArrowUp, Backspace and 's' everywhere on the page while phase==='plan'. It skips only INPUT/TEXTAREA/contentEditable, not BUTTON or A elements. To reproduce at 1280x800 on /kids/play/mars-rover: focus the 'Drive 3 squares' button and press Enter. Nothing is added to the plan (Space does work). Next, add one command, focus the '🗺️ New terrain' button and press Enter. ArrowUp while reading the 1,150-word article does not scroll (scrollY stayed at 2000) and quietly adds '▲' drive commands. Typing 's' anywhere adds Science. Fix: return early when e.target is a BUTTON, A, SELECT or [role=switch|radio], and only take over the arrow keys when the game area has focus or is on screen.
- [HIGH, UX] The results card from finish() is off-screen at both viewports, so the child presses 'Collect my stars' and sees no stars. Measured top edge of the dialog card relative to the viewport: desktop Cadet -79px and Ace -115px (stars and score cut off; see /tmp/mars-rover-qa/shots/d-0-result.png). Fix inside this game: in claim() (MarsRoverGameClient.tsx:279), scroll the game wrapper's top into view before onFinish. Shared fix (needs the shell owner): make the results overlay fixed or sticky to the viewport.
- [HIGH, fairness/difficulty] Legend 3★ cannot be reached on map 2/6 (buildMap(3,1)). sim.ts:1090 uses a fixed 230-point threshold, but target values depend on distance from the start (terrain.ts:341). Map 2's four targets add up to only 195 points (55+55+40+45), against roughly 235–265 on the other maps. A brute-force search of 3,840 single-plan strategies (every target order, rule set and 0–90 min wait) found a best of 227 = 2★ on that map, and 0 strategies reached 3★. On the other maps, 3★ often depends on AEGIS bonus rocks the child cannot see (render.ts draws them only after they are found), so whether you get them comes down to the route, not skill. Fix: scale the star thresholds to the points available on the map (for example a percentage of the sum of target values plus the AEGIS bonus), or normalise target values per map.
- [MEDIUM, challenge] Legend has one recipe that wins, and it ignores the systems the tier is meant to teach. On all six maps the best score comes from one plan: Hazard avoidance + Visual odometry (+ AEGIS), 🧭→target + Science for every target, no Wait, no Power guard, no photos, then End sol. 13–23% of random single-plan strategies get 3★ on maps 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6. The power budget never binds on the best routes (battery ends at 73% on map 1), and the 60 MB passes fit two samples each automatically, so 'Science first' and the Wait/Power guard tips on page.tsx:91 never decide the result. Not 'genuinely hard' for a 16-year-old. Possible fixes: make the battery start lower or add a heater load in the cold morning, make the relay passes tighter than the data the plan produces, and count time in the score.
- [MEDIUM, sim accuracy] sim.ts:1031-1032 isOver() ends a Legend sol as soon as Earth hears 'stuck'. Any samples still queued on the rover are thrown away, even though a stuck rover keeps its radio (Spirit sent science home for about 10 months after it got stuck in 2009, which page.tsx itself says). Reproduce headless: buildMap(3,1), send goto target 3 + Science with haz,vo, then turn and drive into the dune next to it. The mission ends at t=159, before the first relay pass at t=180, with 30 MB still on the rover, giving 0★. The message at sim.ts:1096 then says 'AutoNav can only avoid dunes that are on its map', but the child drove there by hand and the dune is on the orbital map. Fix: in Legend, let relay passes run to timeLimit after 'stuck'; only 'dead' should end the sol early.
- [MEDIUM, mobile usability] At 390x844 the map (canvas top 228px, height 314px) and the plan builder (Drive button at y=1011, Send at y=1347) are never on screen together. The child cannot see the orange preview line while tapping Drive or Turn, so planning means scrolling up and down after every tap (shots/m-3-plan.png, /tmp/mars-rover-qa/shots/qa-m-full.png). ## swarm-reef
- DIFFICULTY ORDER IS INVERTED: Legend is the easiest tier to win, not the hardest. In a headless sweep of 300 random slider settings per tier (sim.ts simulate + scoreRun), the share of runs that won at least 1 star was: Cadet 62%, Pilot 18%, Ace 10%, Legend 40%. The share that won 3 stars was: Cadet 31%, Pilot 2.7%, Ace 0.7%, Legend 3.7%. Following Legend's own hints (rules.tsx:218-223) word for word wins 3 stars on the first try. For example {coh:0, sep:2, explore:1.5, trail:1.5, radius:70} gives 6/6 beacons, 98% searched, 0 collisions at 21.6 s, which is 3 stars and 1340 points, and the browser score matched the headless one. {coh:0, explore:1, trail:1, radius:60} also gives 3 stars. Batteries never matter in a winning run, because the run ends at about 21 s while a battery lasts about 30 s. To make Legend challenge a 16-year-old, tighten the thresholds in sim.ts:1053-1056, shorten battery life (BATTERY_BASE, sim.ts:25), and/or make collisions and the time spent count for more.
- LEGEND STOPS THE RUN BEFORE THE COVERAGE GOAL CAN BE MET: sim.ts:905 sets done as soon as all 6 beacons are found. Coverage is then frozen, even though every robot is still alive and has battery left. Example: {explore:1, trail:1} with other values at default finds 6/6 at 24.3 s with 14/14 robots alive and 10% average battery, but coverage is 85%. That is below the 92% needed for 3 stars (sim.ts:1056), so the run only gets 2 stars. {coh:0, sep:1.5, explore:2, trail:2, radius:60} does the same: 6/6 at 20.3 s, 23% battery left, 85% searched, 2 stars. Finding the beacons quickly is punished, and the brief ('search ... before the batteries run out', rules.tsx:212) is not what actually ends the mission. Either keep running until the batteries or time run out, or drop the coverage condition once all beacons are found.
- SCORING IS NOT DETERMINISTIC, AND THE DEBRIEF CAN DESCRIBE THE WRONG SETTINGS: SwarmReefGameClient.tsx:87-92 writes slider changes into simRef.current.w while a run is going ('You can change them while the swarm swims', :470). With every weight at its maximum, a robot can accelerate at more than 3,000 units/s² against a top speed of 85, which is an almost instant reversal.
