If you or someone in your family has a prostate, hysterectomy or hernia operation this year, there is a fair chance a robot will hold the instruments. The surgeon is still in the room, still making every decision, but their hands are on a console a few metres from the patient rather than inside the incision.

## What it is

A surgical robot is a teleoperated instrument holder, not an autonomous surgeon. The best-known system, Intuitive's da Vinci, has three parts: a surgeon console with a 3D viewer and two hand controllers, a patient-side cart whose arms hold a camera and thin wristed instruments, and a vision tower. Intuitive reported that roughly 3,153,000 procedures were performed with da Vinci systems in 2025, up about 18% on the year before, and that it placed 1,721 systems that year, 870 of them the newest da Vinci 5.

It is no longer a one-company field. SS Innovations in India has installed more than 100 of its SSi Mantra systems across India and six other countries, and Medicaroid in Japan makes the hinotori, approved there for gastrointestinal surgery in October 2022.

## How it works

**Sensing.** A stereo endoscope gives the surgeon a magnified 3D view. The hand controllers measure the surgeon's finger and wrist motion many times a second. The newest generation adds something older systems lacked: da Vinci 5 measures the small forces its instruments exert on tissue and can relay that feeling back into the surgeon's hands, or the surgeon can switch it off.

**Compute.** The controller does three things to the surgeon's motion before it reaches the patient. It scales it down, so a large hand movement becomes a few millimetres at the tip. It filters tremor, so the natural shake in a hand does not reach the tissue (Intuitive's president called da Vinci 5's "unwanted tremor and vibration filtration" the best it has shipped). And it maps the motion through the instrument's kinematics so the tip moves the way the surgeon's wrist moved. Intuitive says da Vinci 5 has 10,000 times more computing power than the previous Xi generation.

**Actuation.** Cables inside each instrument run back to motors on the arm. The wristed tip bends further than a human wrist can, which is the whole point: through a keyhole incision, a human hand holding a straight laparoscopic tool cannot turn a corner. The robot's wrist can.

## Where you'll see it

- **United States and worldwide.** da Vinci is the installed base nearly everywhere robotic surgery is done; the 2025 numbers above are global. In preclinical tests, surgeons using da Vinci 5's force-feedback instruments exerted significantly less force on tissue than with the Xi, though Intuitive has said clinical studies are still needed to show that means less injury.
- **India.** SSi Mantra had been used in more than 5,000 procedures by July 2025, including 240 cardiac surgeries and 32 telesurgeries. In one case a surgeon in Gurugram operated on a patient in Indore, about 560 miles away. The company's stated goal is to make robotic surgery affordable for a larger share of the world's patients.
- **Japan.** At Fujita Health University's Okazaki Medical Center, surgeons reported their first ten liver resections with the hinotori between August 2023 and October 2024. Median operation time was 419.5 minutes, one case was converted to open surgery, and all tumour cases achieved clean margins. The authors' conclusion was modest and honest: the operation "was feasibly performed."

## What it can't do yet

It does not operate on its own. Every movement is the surgeon's; the robot removes tremor and scales motion, nothing more. Until da Vinci 5, surgeons got no sense of touch at all and judged tissue tension by watching it deform on screen, and even now force feedback is new and its clinical benefit is unproven. Operations with the robot often take longer than open surgery, as the Fujita numbers show. The systems are expensive to buy and maintain, which is why a 100-system installed base in India is news and why remote surgery across a country is still a demonstration rather than routine care. And the patient's outcome still depends mostly on the person at the console.
