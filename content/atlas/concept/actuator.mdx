An **actuator** is the part of a machine that turns a control signal into physical movement — force, torque or displacement. If [sensors](/atlas/concept/sensor) are a robot's senses, actuators are its muscles.

Two separate things always go into an actuator: a **control signal**, which carries almost no energy (a voltage, a current, a pressure command), and a **source of energy**, which does the actual work. That split is the whole trick: a signal your controller produces for a fraction of a watt ends up commanding a motion that lifts a car door.

## The actuator families

| Family | Energy source | Strength | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electric (rotary) | Electricity | Precise, quiet, easy to control and power | Needs gearing to turn fast rotation into useful force |
| Electromechanical (linear) | Electricity | Good accuracy and long life; forces on the order of 100 kN | Limited by speed, size and weight |
| Linear motor | Electricity | Very high speed and accuracy, no gears | Suited to light loads (around 30 kg); low force density; expensive |
| Hydraulic | Pressurised liquid | Huge force; holds position with little energy; low inertia | Limited acceleration; pumps, hoses and leaks |
| Pneumatic | Compressed gas | Simple, light, fast, no return pipes needed | Needs compressors, reservoirs, filters and air treatment |
| Thermal / shape-memory | Heat | Tiny, no moving parts to wear | Slow, hard to control finely |
| Piezoelectric | Electricity | Sub-nanometre positioning, microsecond response | Travel measured in tens of micrometres |

Electric actuators cover almost everything a beginner will build: a [DC motor](/atlas/concept/dc-motor), a [stepper motor](/atlas/concept/stepper-motor), a [servo motor](/atlas/concept/servo-motor). Add gearing — a [harmonic drive](/atlas/concept/harmonic-drive), say — and the same motor becomes strong and slow instead of weak and fast. Add an [encoder](/atlas/concept/encoder) and the controller knows where the output actually is, which is the difference between "turn for 300 ms" and "go to 42 degrees and stay there."

[Hydraulic actuators](/atlas/concept/hydraulic-actuator) win wherever brute force matters, because a liquid is nearly incompressible: the pressure you apply goes into the piston rather than being soaked up. [Pneumatic actuators](/atlas/concept/pneumatic-actuator) swap the oil for air, which makes them cheap, clean and quick — and much harder to stop in a precise place, because air springs back.

## Specs that matter

- **Torque** — the rotational force available. For scale, a Dynamixel XM430-W350 smart servo is rated at 4.1 N·m stall torque at 12 V and weighs 82 g.
- **Speed** — revolutions per minute for a motor, millimetres per second for a linear actuator. The same XM430-W350 spins at 46 rev/min with no load; torque and speed always trade against each other.
- **Resolution** — the smallest commanded step. That servo reports 4096 positions per revolution.
- **Backlash** — the dead play when reversing direction, almost all of it from the gearbox rather than the motor.
- **Repeatability** — whether commanding the same position twice lands the output in the same place twice. This, not top speed, is what makes an arm useful.

Software treats an actuator as a unit too. In `ros2_control`, an [Actuator](https://control.ros.org/humble/doc/getting_started/getting_started.html) is a single-degree-of-freedom hardware component bound to one joint, exposing a command interface such as `position` and, where the hardware can report it, state interfaces for position and velocity.


## Common questions

### Is a motor the same thing as an actuator?

A motor is one kind of actuator — the most common kind. "Actuator" is the wider word, and it also covers hydraulic and pneumatic cylinders, shape-memory wires, piezo stacks and solenoids. In practice an actuator is often a motor plus a gearbox, a sensor and a driver, sold as one part.

### How is an actuator different from a sensor?

They run in opposite directions. A sensor converts something physical into a signal; an actuator converts a signal into something physical. Both are transducers, and a complete robot needs each: sense, decide, act.

### Which type should a beginner start with?

A hobby servo or a smart servo. It arrives with its own gearbox, position feedback and driver, so you command an angle instead of designing a control loop — then move on to bare DC motors, closed-loop control and a [gripper](/atlas/concept/gripper).
