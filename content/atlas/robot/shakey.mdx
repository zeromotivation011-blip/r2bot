Shakey was a wheeled research robot developed at SRI (then Stanford Research Institute) in California between roughly 1966 and 1972. It was called Shakey because it wobbled as it moved.

## Why it was special

Shakey was among the first robots that could reason about its own actions. You could give it a goal such as "push the box to the other room," and it would work out a plan: find the door, push the box through, and so on. It used a camera, a range finder and bump sensors.

## Famous outcomes

Work on Shakey produced ideas that robots still use:

- **A\* search**, a method to find the shortest path through a map. It is used in games, maps and [path planning](/atlas/concept/path-planning).
- **STRIPS**, an early way to describe actions and goals so that a computer can plan.

## What it could not do

Shakey was very slow. Its computer was in another room and took long to think, so a single move could take minutes. It worked only in simple rooms with plain objects.

## Lesson

Modern robots are faster because of better computers and sensors, but they still follow the same loop Shakey used: sense, plan, act.
