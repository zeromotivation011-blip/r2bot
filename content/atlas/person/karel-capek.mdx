Karel Čapek (1890 to 1938) was a Czech writer. His 1920 play *R.U.R.* (Rossum's Universal Robots) is where the word "robot" first reached a wide audience.

## Where the word comes from

"Robot" comes from the Czech word *robota*, meaning forced labour. Čapek credited his brother, the artist Josef Čapek, with suggesting the word.

## The play

In *R.U.R.*, artificial workers are made in a factory. They are not metal machines but biological, humanlike beings. The story asks what happens when workers are treated as objects, and it ends badly for the humans.

## Why it matters

The word started in fiction, not in a lab. Writers like Čapek and [Isaac Asimov](/atlas/person/isaac-asimov) shaped how the public imagines robots, which still affects how we talk about them today.
