# What Is SLAM? A Plain-Words Guide to How Robots Map and Find Themselves

**Short answer:** **SLAM** stands for Simultaneous Localization And Mapping. A robot dropped in an unknown place builds a map of it while also working out where it is on that map. See [SLAM](/atlas/concept/slam) in the Atlas.

## Why it is a chicken-and-egg problem

To draw a good map you need to know exactly where you are. To know where you are you need a good map. SLAM solves both together, improving each estimate using the other.

## How it works, simply

1. The robot moves and estimates how far it went, using wheel counts ([odometry](/atlas/concept/odometry)). This drifts over time.
2. It scans the surroundings, often with [lidar](/atlas/concept/lidar) or a camera.
3. It matches the new scan with the map so far, and corrects its position.
4. When it returns to a place it has seen before, it snaps the map shut. That is [loop closure](/atlas/concept/loop-closure).

## Everyday example

Walk through a new building with your eyes closed, counting steps. After a while you are unsure where you are. Open your eyes at a landmark and you instantly fix your position. SLAM does the same with sensors.

## Where it is used

- Robot vacuums mapping your home.
- Warehouse robots moving between shelves.
- Drones flying indoors without GPS.
- Phone augmented reality apps.

## Try it

In ROS 2, navigation packages such as [Nav2](/atlas/concept/nav2) work with maps made by SLAM. The [lidar SLAM](/atlas/concept/lidar-slam) entry shows a typical setup.

## FAQ

**Does SLAM need GPS?** No, which is why it works indoors.

**Does it need lidar?** No. Camera-based variants exist, called visual SLAM.

**Is SLAM solved?** It works well in many settings, but crowds, mirrors and changing scenes still cause trouble.
