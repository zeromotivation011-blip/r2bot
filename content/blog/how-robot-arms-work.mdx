# How Robot Arms Work

A robot arm is a chain of rigid links connected by joints, with a tool at the end. Your own arm is a good model: shoulder, elbow, wrist, hand.

## The parts

- **Links:** the rigid sections.
- **Joints:** where links turn or slide. Each joint is driven by a motor, often with gears. See [motors for robots](/blog/motors-for-robots-dc-servo-stepper).
- **End effector:** the tool at the tip, usually a [gripper](/atlas/concept/gripper) but sometimes a welder, a paint gun or a suction cup.
- **Sensors:** encoders at each joint tell the controller its angle.

## Degrees of freedom

A [degree of freedom](/atlas/concept/degrees-of-freedom) (DOF) is one independent way the arm can move. To place a tool anywhere in space and point it in any direction you need six: three for position and three for orientation. That is why many industrial arms have six joints. Simple hobby arms have three or four and are more limited.

## Forward kinematics

If you know every joint angle, you can calculate where the tip is. This is [forward kinematics](/atlas/concept/forward-kinematics), and it is basic geometry (sines and cosines of the angles).

## Inverse kinematics

The harder, more useful question: I want the tip at this point, so what angles should each joint take? That is [inverse kinematics](/atlas/concept/inverse-kinematics). There may be several answers, or none if the point is out of reach. Software solves this many times per second.

## Why grasping is hard

Picking up a known box in a fixed position is easy. Picking up different objects from a messy bin is hard, because the robot must see the object, decide how to hold it, and judge force. Vision and [force sensing](/atlas/concept/force-sensor) help. This is why many warehouses still use humans for picking.

## Types of arms

- **Articulated arms:** rotating joints like a human arm; the most common industrial type.
- **SCARA arms:** fast, flat-moving arms used for assembly.
- **Cartesian (gantry) robots:** move along straight axes like a 3D printer.
- **Parallel robots:** several arms linked to one platform, very fast for light parts.

See [industrial robot](/atlas/concept/industrial-robot) and [robot arm](/atlas/concept/robot-arm).

## What goes wrong

- If the arm is asked to reach beyond its limits, the maths has no solution and it stops or jams.
- Gears can have backlash, small loose play that makes precise positioning harder.
- Without enough torque, a joint sags under load.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many joints should a beginner arm have?**
Three or four servos is enough to learn the idea.

**Is inverse kinematics hard?**
For simple arms it is a small amount of trigonometry. Libraries handle harder cases.

**What is a cobot arm?**
An arm built to work safely next to people. See [collaborative robot safety](/atlas/concept/collaborative-robot-safety).
