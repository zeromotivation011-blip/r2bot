# The Sim-to-Real Gap: Why Robots Trained in Simulation Fail in Real Life

**Short answer:** a robot trained in a simulator learns the simulator's physics, which is never exactly the real world's. The mismatch is the **sim-to-real gap**. Engineers shrink it with tricks such as [domain randomization](/atlas/concept/domain-randomization). See [sim-to-real transfer](/atlas/concept/sim-to-real-transfer).

## Why simulate at all?

- **Speed:** millions of practice runs in hours.
- **Safety:** a falling robot costs nothing in simulation.
- **Cost:** no broken motors.

## Where the gap comes from

- Friction, motor lag and sensor noise are approximated.
- Camera images in simulation look cleaner than real ones.
- Small timing delays that do not exist in the simulator.

## Example

A simulated robot hand learns to rotate a block perfectly. On the real hand, the block slips because real fingertips are slightly stickier than the model. The policy never saw that.

## How it is reduced

1. **Domain randomization:** randomise friction, mass, lighting and delays during training so the policy copes with variety.
2. **Better models:** measure the real robot and tune the simulator to match.
3. **Fine-tuning on real data:** a short real-world training stage at the end.
4. **Safety layers:** classic controllers that limit unsafe actions.

## Without these steps

A policy that scores 100% in simulation may fall over immediately on hardware, which wastes weeks of training. Test on the real robot early and often.

## FAQ

**Can the gap be removed completely?** Not really. The goal is to make the policy robust to it.

**Which simulators are common?** Several physics simulators are used in robotics research and industry. Start with whichever your robot framework supports.
