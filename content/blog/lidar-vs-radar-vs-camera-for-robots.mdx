# LiDAR vs Radar vs Camera: Which Sensor Does a Robot Need?

**Short answer:** a **camera** is cheap and tells you *what* something is, a **lidar** tells you precisely *where* things are in 3D, and **radar** works in rain, fog and dark and measures speed directly. Most serious robots combine them, which is called [sensor fusion](/atlas/concept/sensor-fusion).

## At a glance

| | Camera | Lidar | Radar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Measures | Light (colour images) | Laser pulse timing (distance) | Radio wave echoes (distance and speed) |
| Strength | Recognises objects, signs, people; low cost | Accurate shape and distance | Works in bad weather; measures speed |
| Weakness | Poor in dark or glare; no direct distance | Costs more; can struggle with rain, fog and glass | Coarse detail |
| Typical use | Object detection, lane finding | Mapping, obstacle avoidance | Collision warning, long range detection |

Read more: [camera vision](/atlas/concept/camera-vision), [lidar](/atlas/concept/lidar), [radar](/atlas/concept/radar-sensor).

## Which one to choose

- **Learning or a hobby robot:** start with a camera and a cheap distance sensor.
- **Indoor mapping robot:** a 2D lidar is the classic choice and pairs with [SLAM](/atlas/concept/slam).
- **Outdoor robot or vehicle:** add radar because it keeps working when cameras and lidar are degraded.

## Why not just one?

Each sensor has blind spots in different conditions. A camera can be blinded by sun glare, and radar cannot read a road sign. Fusing them lets the strengths cover the weaknesses. Some companies deliberately use fewer sensor types to cut cost, and the right mix depends on the job and budget.

## FAQ

**Which is cheapest?** Cameras, by a wide margin.

**Can a camera measure distance?** Yes, using two cameras (stereo) or software estimates, but it is less direct than lidar or radar.

**What is the best sensor for a beginner robot?** A camera plus an ultrasonic or infrared distance sensor.
