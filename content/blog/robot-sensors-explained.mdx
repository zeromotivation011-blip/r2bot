# Robot Sensors Explained

A robot cannot act sensibly if it cannot sense. Sensors turn the real world into numbers a program can use. Here are the main kinds, in plain language.

## Distance sensors

**Ultrasonic sensors** send out a burst of sound above human hearing and time the echo. Longer echo means farther object. The common HC-SR04 is cheap and perfect for beginners. It struggles with soft surfaces that absorb sound and with angled surfaces that bounce it away. See [ultrasonic sensor](/atlas/concept/ultrasonic-sensor).

**Infrared (IR) sensors** use light. They work well at short range and as line sensors, but bright sunlight can confuse them.

**[Lidar](/atlas/concept/lidar)** spins a laser to measure distance in many directions, producing a map-like scan. It is accurate but costs more.

## Vision sensors

**Cameras** give rich information: colour, shape, text. Software must interpret the image, which is harder. **[Depth cameras](/atlas/concept/depth-camera)** also give distance for each pixel. See [Computer Vision for Robots](/blog/computer-vision-for-robots-explained).

## Motion and orientation sensors

An **[IMU](/atlas/concept/imu)** (inertial measurement unit) measures acceleration and rotation. It tells a robot if it is tilting or turning. Balancing robots and drones depend on it. Its weakness is drift: small errors add up over time.

## Position sensors

An **[encoder](/atlas/concept/encoder)** counts how much a wheel or joint has turned. With it, a robot can drive a set distance or hold a joint angle. Wheels can slip, so encoders alone can drift from the truth.

## Touch and force sensors

Bump switches tell a robot it has hit something. [Force sensors](/atlas/concept/force-sensor) measure how hard a gripper squeezes, which lets a robot hold an egg without crushing it.

## Why robots use several sensors together

Each sensor has weak spots. Combining them, called **sensor fusion**, gives a more reliable picture. A self-driving car mixes cameras, lidar and radar for this reason. See [LiDAR vs Radar vs Camera](/blog/lidar-vs-radar-vs-camera-for-robots).

## What goes wrong with the wrong sensor

- An ultrasonic sensor on a robot driving over a fluffy carpet may read nothing at all.
- A camera-only robot struggles in the dark.
- A robot that relies only on wheel encoders will slowly lose track of where it is.

## Quick picker for beginners

| I want my robot to... | Use |
|---|---|
| Avoid walls | Ultrasonic or IR distance sensor |
| Follow a line | Two or more IR line sensors |
| Know how far it drove | Wheel encoders |
| Stay balanced | IMU |
| Recognise objects | Camera + Raspberry Pi |

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best sensor for a beginner?**
The ultrasonic sensor: cheap, easy, and teaches distance sensing.

**Can a robot work with just one sensor?**
Yes, for simple tasks. Complex tasks need several.

**What is sensor noise?**
Random error in a reading. Good code averages or filters readings to reduce its effect.
