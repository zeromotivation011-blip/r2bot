# How Do Robots Work? The Sense-Think-Act Loop in Plain English

**Short answer:** a robot repeats a loop thousands of times a second: it **senses** the world, **thinks** about what to do, then **acts**. Sensors are the eyes and ears, a computer is the brain, and motors are the muscles. See the [Sense-Think-Act loop](/atlas/concept/sense-think-act-loop) in the Atlas for the formal version.

## The three steps

**1. Sense.** Sensors turn the world into numbers. A [lidar](/atlas/concept/lidar) measures distance with laser pulses, a camera captures images, an [encoder](/atlas/concept/encoder) counts wheel turns.

**2. Think.** Software decides what to do with those numbers. It might be a few lines ("if the wall is closer than 20 cm, turn") or a large [neural network](/atlas/concept/neural-network).

**3. Act.** The computer commands [actuators](/atlas/concept/actuator), such as a [DC motor](/atlas/concept/dc-motor), to move. The movement changes the world, and the loop starts again.

## A robot vacuum, step by step

- **Sense:** bump sensors and a distance sensor notice a chair leg.
- **Think:** the software marks the spot on its map and picks a path around it.
- **Act:** the wheel motors steer left.

Half a second later it senses the new position, and the loop repeats.

## Why the loop matters

Reality is messy: wheels slip, sensors are noisy, people walk in the way. Because the robot keeps sensing, it can correct itself. This idea is called [feedback](/atlas/concept/feedback). A robot without it is just a machine replaying a recording, which works only until something changes.

## What goes wrong without it

A toy that drives forward for exactly 3 seconds will hit the wall if the floor is slippery. A robot that senses distance will stop in time. That difference is the whole point of sensing.

## FAQ

**Is a thermostat a robot?** It senses and acts, but most definitions also want a physical body that does work in the world and some decision-making. It is a simple control system, not usually called a robot.

**Do robots need AI?** No. Many industrial robots follow fixed programs. AI helps when the world is unpredictable, for example recognising objects.

**What is the best way to start learning?** Build the loop yourself in a simulator, then on a small board such as an [Arduino](/atlas/concept/arduino). Try Lesson 1 of the free Spark track in the [Academy](/academy).
