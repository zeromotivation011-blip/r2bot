# How Robots Learn: Machine Learning, Imitation and Reinforcement Learning

**Short answer:** robots learn in three main ways: from **examples** (supervised [machine learning](/atlas/concept/machine-learning)), by **copying a demonstration** ([imitation learning](/atlas/concept/imitation-learning)), or by **trial and error with a score** ([reinforcement learning](/atlas/concept/reinforcement-learning)).

## 1. Learning from labelled examples

Show the system thousands of photos labelled "cup" or "not a cup" and it learns to spot cups. This powers [object detection](/atlas/concept/object-detection). **Good for:** perception. **Weak spot:** it needs lots of labelled data.

## 2. Learning by imitation

A person guides the robot arm through a task, and the robot learns to repeat it. **Good for:** manipulation tasks that are easy to demonstrate. **Weak spot:** it can struggle when the situation differs from the demos.

## 3. Learning by trial and reward

The robot tries actions, gets a **reward** when it does well, and gradually prefers actions that earn more. A simulated walking robot might get points for staying upright and moving forward. The reward design matters a lot: see [reward shaping](/atlas/concept/reward-shaping).

- **Without a well-designed reward:** the robot may find a shortcut, like falling forward to "move forward" quickly.
- **With good shaping:** it learns the behaviour you actually wanted.

## Why simulation is used

Trial and error on real hardware is slow and breaks things. So robots often practise millions of times in simulation first. The catch is the [sim-to-real gap](/blog/sim-to-real-gap-explained).

## Choosing a method

| If you have | Consider |
|---|---|
| Lots of labelled images | Supervised learning |
| A human who can demonstrate | Imitation learning |
| A good simulator and a clear score | Reinforcement learning |

## FAQ

**Can a robot learn on its own in the real world?** Yes, but slowly and with safety limits, so most training happens in simulation first.

**Is deep learning the same thing?** [Deep learning](/atlas/concept/deep-learning) is a technique that can be used inside all three approaches.
