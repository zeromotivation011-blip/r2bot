# Agility Robotics becomes the first US humanoid company on the stock market

Agility Robotics, the Oregon company behind the two-legged [Digit](/atlas/robot/digit) robot, began trading on the Nasdaq stock exchange on 5 November 2026 under the ticker AGLT. Bloomberg describes it as the first humanoid-focused company to go public in the United States.

## How it happened

Agility did not do a traditional IPO. It merged with a listed shell company called Churchill Capital Corp XI, a route known as a SPAC. Bloomberg reports the company expects to have about $465 million in cash before expenses, including roughly $201 million of new investment led by Foxconn. Other backers named include Nvidia, Amazon and SoftBank Vision Fund 2.

## The reality check

TechSpot reports that Agility made only about $1.8 million in revenue last year. That is a very small number next to the valuation attached to the deal. In plain words: investors are betting on the future of humanoid robots, not on today's sales.

The same Bloomberg report notes that Chinese humanoid maker [Unitree](/atlas/company/unitree) listed in Shanghai in August, so Agility is not the first in the world, only the first in the US.

## What Digit actually does

Digit's work today is simple: moving totes and boxes in warehouses. Agility is expanding its relationship with the logistics company GXO.

## Why you should care

If you are learning robotics, this is a signal about where jobs may appear. Companies that build legged robots need people who understand control, perception and safety. But treat the hype with care: a stock listing is not proof that a robot is useful at scale.
