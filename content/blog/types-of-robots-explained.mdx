# Types of Robots Explained

There are many kinds of robots, but most fall into a handful of families. Knowing the families helps you understand news, pick a career path, and decide what to build first.

## The short answer

The main types are **industrial arms**, **collaborative robots (cobots)**, **mobile robots**, **legged and humanoid robots**, **drones and flying robots**, **medical and surgical robots**, **space robots**, and **home and service robots**.

## 1. Industrial robot arms

A fixed arm with several joints that welds, paints, lifts or assembles. It repeats precise movements all day. Companies such as [FANUC](/atlas/company/fanuc) and [KUKA](/atlas/company/kuka) built this field, and the first one, [Unimate](/atlas/robot/unimate), started work in 1961. See [industrial robot](/atlas/concept/industrial-robot) and [robot arm](/atlas/concept/robot-arm).

**Strength:** speed, precision, reliability. **Limit:** usually needs a safety cage and a fixed task.

## 2. Collaborative robots (cobots)

Arms designed to work next to people without a cage. They sense contact and limit force. [Universal Robots](/atlas/company/universal-robots) made them popular. See [cobot safety](/atlas/concept/collaborative-robot-safety).

**Strength:** flexible and easy to set up. **Limit:** slower than caged robots.

## 3. Mobile robots

Robots that drive around: warehouse carts, delivery bots, robot vacuums like the [Roomba](/atlas/robot/roomba). They need to know where they are, so they rely on sensors and [SLAM](/blog/what-is-slam-in-plain-words). See [mobile robot](/atlas/concept/mobile-robot).

**Strength:** cheap and practical on flat floors. **Limit:** stairs and rough terrain.

## 4. Legged and humanoid robots

Robots with legs, such as the four-legged [Spot](/atlas/robot/spot) or the two-legged [Digit](/atlas/robot/digit). Legs go where wheels cannot, but they are harder to balance and more expensive. See [humanoid robot](/atlas/concept/humanoid-robot).

**Strength:** works in spaces built for people. **Limit:** cost, battery life and safety are still being solved.

## 5. Drones and flying robots

Small flying machines for photography, inspection, farming and delivery. Even Mars has had one: [Ingenuity](/atlas/robot/ingenuity-helicopter).

## 6. Medical and surgical robots

Robots that help doctors, such as the [da Vinci system](/atlas/robot/da-vinci-surgical-system), where a surgeon controls robot arms from a console. Others assist with rehabilitation or move supplies in hospitals.

## 7. Space robots

Rovers and landers explore other worlds, such as [Curiosity](/atlas/robot/curiosity-rover) and [Perseverance](/atlas/robot/perseverance-rover). They are semi-autonomous because radio signals take minutes to travel.

## 8. Home and service robots

Vacuum robots, lawn mowers, window cleaners, restaurant servers, social robots. They succeed when they do one job cheaply.

## Which type should you learn first?

Start with a **mobile robot** (a small wheeled car with sensors). It teaches sensing, control and programming with a low-cost kit. From there, move to arms or vision. See [How Do Robots Work?](/blog/how-do-robots-work) and [our beginner guide](/blog/robotics-for-students-india-beginners-guide).

## What goes wrong if you choose by hype

Humanoid robots get the headlines, but most robots earning money today are arms and wheeled machines. If you aim for a job, learn the basics that apply to all types: sensors, control, and software.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the most common type of robot?**
Industrial robot arms and robot vacuums are among the most common, by number working today.

**Is a drone a robot?**
Yes, if it senses its surroundings and controls itself to some degree. A remote-controlled toy with no sensing is closer to a toy.

**Are humanoids the future?**
They are one possibility, especially where spaces are built for humans, but wheeled and specialised robots will remain cheaper for many jobs.
