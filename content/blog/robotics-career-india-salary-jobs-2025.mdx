# Robotics Careers: Jobs, Skills and How to Get In

"How much does a robotics engineer earn?" is the most common question in robotics communities. The honest answer: it depends on the country, the company and the specialty, and published numbers go out of date quickly. This guide focuses on what you control: which skills are valued, who hires, and how to build a path.

## How to find real pay numbers for your country

Don't trust a single blog. Check at least three sources:

1. **Current job listings.** Many list a salary range. Search "robotics engineer" in your city.
2. **Salary reports.** Look for recent, country-specific surveys and public salary-sharing sites.
3. **People in the job.** Ask two or three engineers, politely, what a fair range is for your level.

Typical patterns hold almost everywhere: entry-level pay is modest, pay rises steeply once you own a real subsystem, and specialists in perception, controls and navigation are paid more than generalists.

## Who hires robotics people

- **Product companies.** Warehouse robots, factory automation, delivery robots, surgical and medical robots, agriculture robots, drones.
- **The big robot makers.** Industrial arm manufacturers hire controls and integration engineers in many countries.
- **Research labs and universities.** Slower pay growth, big problems, long projects.
- **Government and space agencies.** Stable roles, often more bureaucratic.
- **Big tech and AI labs.** Perception, simulation and learning-based robotics.
- **IT services companies** building robotics practices. Often easier to enter.

See [Robotics Startups to Know](/blog/india-robotics-startups-2025) for the kinds of company to look for.

## Skills that employers value most

In rough order of hiring demand:

1. **ROS 2 and robot software.** Writing nodes in C++ and Python. See [ROS vs ROS 2](/blog/ros-vs-ros2-which-to-learn).
2. **Perception and computer vision.** OpenCV, PyTorch, sensor fusion. See [Computer Vision for Robots](/blog/computer-vision-for-robots-explained).
3. **Embedded C/C++.** Firmware, real-time control, motor drivers.
4. **Navigation and [SLAM](/atlas/concept/slam).** Mobile robot autonomy is in high demand.
5. **Mechanical design and simulation.** CAD plus analysis, with a real portfolio.

The pattern that works: pick two, and go genuinely deep. One subsystem you built end to end is worth more than ten résumé bullets.

## A path from student to professional

**School years.** Build one published project, such as a line follower with [PID](/atlas/concept/pid-controller) tuning. Enter one competition.

**University, years 1 and 2.** Join a robotics club. Take ownership of one sub-team (motors, vision or controls). Put the result in a portfolio.

**University, year 3.** Apply for internships. One real internship is worth more than ten online courses.

**Final year.** Apply broadly. Strong candidates have three robots they truly built, depth in one skill stack, and clean code with version control.

**First years at work.** Own a significant subsystem. Practise saying "I built X end to end and chose this trade-off because..." That sentence changes your next job offer.

## What the work is really like

Most days are code, debugging hardware, running simulations, design reviews, and explaining results to non-engineers. Demos are about 10% of the work. The rest is careful, repeated engineering.

## What goes wrong

Spreading yourself across every buzzword. Hiring managers see "knows ROS, ML, CAD, 5G, blockchain" and cannot tell what you can actually do. Depth in one or two areas, backed by something you built, beats a long list of keywords.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a degree?**
Most roles ask for one in engineering or computer science, but proven projects can open doors, especially at smaller companies.

**Is robotics a stable career?**
Demand is growing in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare and agriculture, but individual companies come and go. Transferable skills (embedded, perception, software) protect you.

**Which country is best?**
Robotics jobs exist worldwide. Look at where your preferred industry is concentrated, and consider remote roles.
