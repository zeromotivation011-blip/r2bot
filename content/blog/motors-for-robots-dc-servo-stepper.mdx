# DC Motor vs Servo vs Stepper: Which Motor Should Your Robot Use?

Motors are the muscles of a robot. The three you meet first are **DC motors**, **servos** and **steppers**. Each is good at one thing.

## Quick answer

- **Wheels that spin continuously:** DC gear motor.
- **A joint that must point at an exact angle:** servo.
- **Precise positioning without feedback, such as a 3D printer axis:** stepper.

## DC motor

A [DC motor](/atlas/concept/dc-motor) spins when you apply voltage. More voltage means more speed. To control speed you switch the power on and off very fast, called [PWM](/atlas/concept/pwm), usually through a [motor driver](/atlas/concept/motor-driver).

**Good for:** driving wheels, fans, pumps. **Weak at:** knowing how far it has turned. Add an [encoder](/atlas/concept/encoder) to count rotations.

**If you choose wrong:** using a DC motor for an arm joint means it will drift, because nothing tells it where to stop.

## Servo motor

A hobby [servo](/atlas/concept/servo-motor) has a motor, gears and a small circuit inside. You send it a target angle and it moves there and holds. Most hobby servos turn only part of a full circle.

**Good for:** robot arm joints, grippers, steering, pan-tilt camera mounts. **Weak at:** continuous spinning and heavy loads on cheap models.

**If you choose wrong:** using a small servo for a heavy joint makes it overheat and jitter.

## Stepper motor

A [stepper motor](/atlas/concept/stepper-motor) moves in tiny fixed steps. If you command 200 steps, it moves 200 steps, without needing a sensor in many cases. That is why 3D printers and CNC machines use them.

**Good for:** accurate positioning, slow smooth motion. **Weak at:** high speed and efficiency. They draw current even when they are holding still.

**If you choose wrong:** if a stepper is overloaded it can skip steps, and the robot will not know its position was lost.

## Comparison

| | DC gear motor | Servo | Stepper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moves by | Continuous spin | Goes to an angle | Fixed steps |
| Position known? | No (add encoder) | Yes, built in | Usually, if not overloaded |
| Needs driver | Yes | No (plugs into a pin) | Yes |
| Typical use | Wheels | Arm joints | 3D printers |
| Beginner difficulty | Easy | Easiest | Medium |

## Brushless motors

Drones and advanced robots use [brushless motors](/atlas/concept/brushless-motor), which are efficient and powerful but need special controllers. Leave these until you are comfortable with the basics. The hobby entry point is a small drone motor with an electronic speed controller; the same motor family drives the joints of modern legged robots, where high torque with low weight matters most.

## What to buy first

For a first robot car, buy two DC gear motors and an L298N motor driver. Add one servo to turn a distance sensor. This is exactly the setup in our [first robot tutorial](/blog/first-robot-project-india-step-by-step).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a servo as a wheel motor?**
Only if it is a continuous-rotation servo, which spins instead of holding angles.

**Do I need an encoder?**
For a simple robot, no. For driving straight or measuring distance, yes.

**Why is my motor weak or stalling?**
Check battery voltage and current, and remember that motors need more current than a microcontroller pin can give.
