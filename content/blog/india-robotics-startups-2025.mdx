# Robotics Startups to Know: What They Build and How to Get Hired

Most robotics jobs are not at the famous humanoid labs. They are at small and mid-size companies solving one boring, valuable problem: moving boxes, inspecting pipes, testing phones, cleaning dangerous places. This guide explains the main kinds of robotics startups, the roles they hire for, and what actually gets a beginner noticed.

## The five kinds of robotics startups

**1. Warehouse and logistics robots.** Mobile robots that carry shelves, sort parcels or pick items. This is the biggest hiring area because every online store needs faster shipping. Examples from around the world include GreyOrange (India and the US), Addverb (India), and many European and US companies. Work here leans on [SLAM](/atlas/concept/slam), fleet software and safety.

**2. Factory and industrial robots.** Arms, inspection robots and autonomous vehicles for factory floors. Ati Motors (India) builds indoor industrial vehicles, and Sastra Robotics (India) builds robot arms that test electronics. Work here leans on reliability, safety standards and motion control.

**3. Inspection and field robots.** Robots that go where people should not: pipes, tanks, mines, power plants. GridBots (India) is one example. These jobs are stable and less hyped.

**4. Medical robots.** Surgical assistants, rehabilitation devices, screening tools. Hiring is slower and more regulated, but the work is meaningful.

**5. Service and social robots.** Robots in airports, hospitals and hotels. Invento Robotics (India) makes the Mitra social robot, and Genrobotics (India) built Bandicoot, a robot for cleaning manholes. These companies value people who can combine hardware, software and a good user experience.

> Tip: ignore the list of names and look for the kind of problem you like. Search "[problem] robot startup" in your own country; there is almost always something nearby.

## Roles that actually exist

1. **Robotics software engineer.** C++ or Python on Linux, often [ROS 2](/atlas/concept/ros2), plus simulation. The most common opening.
2. **Embedded systems engineer.** Firmware, motor drivers, real-time control on chips like STM32 or ESP32.
3. **Computer vision / perception engineer.** Cameras, [lidar](/atlas/concept/lidar), and machine learning.
4. **Controls engineer.** [PID](/atlas/concept/pid-controller), model-predictive control, motion planning. Rarer and well paid.
5. **Mechanical engineer.** CAD, design for manufacturing, materials.
6. **Test and QA for robots.** Growing quickly as products move from demo to daily use.

Pay differs a lot by country and company, so check current listings and salary reports for your region instead of trusting any single number.

## How to get noticed (from anywhere)

- **Show one working robot.** A short video of something you built beats a long list of courses. Put the code on GitHub with a clear README.
- **Go deep in one stack.** Being excellent at embedded C++ or at ROS 2 navigation beats being average at everything.
- **Message engineers, not recruiters.** Ask one specific question about their product, then ask if they take interns. Keep it short and polite.
- **Internships are the back door.** Many full-time hires at small robotics companies started as interns. Apply early in the year.
- **Explain trade-offs in plain words.** Interviewers like people who can say why they chose a sensor or a controller.

## Where to look

- The careers pages of companies you like
- Professional networks (filter for "robotics")
- Startup job boards
- University lab pages and open-source robotics communities, which often lead to jobs
