On 5 November 2026 the Financial Times reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that China's top economic planning agency told SoftBank Group it will not approve SoftBank's planned $5.4 billion purchase of ABB's robotics division.

## What is reported

Coverage of the story says Beijing is worried that robot data collected in China could leave the country and be used to train AI models, including those developed by OpenAI, in which SoftBank is a major investor. One summary puts China at roughly 30 percent of the robotics business.

## What is not confirmed

None of the reports included a statement from SoftBank, ABB or the Chinese government. The deal was subject to regulatory approvals and had been expected to close in mid-to-late 2026. Until there is an official statement, this remains a media report.

## Why it matters for robotics

Robots are no longer just machines. They are data collectors. A factory robot with cameras generates information about products, processes and workers. Governments are starting to treat that data as a strategic asset. That connects robotics to [AI](/blog/ai-and-robotics-complete-guide): the more real-world data a company gets, the better its models can become.

For learners, this is a reminder that robotics sits between engineering, business and politics. The big industrial robot makers, such as [FANUC](/atlas/company/fanuc), [KUKA](/atlas/company/kuka) and [Universal Robots](/atlas/company/universal-robots), operate in global markets that can change with a single regulatory decision.
